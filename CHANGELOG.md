## [0.1.0] - 2026-11-27

### Added

- The foundation of the rebuilt Cuisine: a FastAPI backend in hexagonal layers and a Vue 3 frontend with the OpenVue look, in German and English.
- Accounts created by an administrator, freely named groups as permission bundles (a fresh install starts with Cooks, Kitchen helpers, Curators, Editors, Administrators and the sharing circles Family and Friends), and a system administrator seeded from the `INIT__` settings and synced back to them on every start, with mandatory two-factor authentication and sessions that end 15 minutes after sign-in.
- Sign-in with the e-mail address and a password (there are no usernames; the system administrator signs in as `system`), TOTP two-factor authentication with trusted devices and forced enrolment, and passkeys; login throttling, server-side sessions that can be listed and revoked, and a security log.
- Personal access tokens and API keys with their own permission sets.
- Admin settings for the instance name and default language, the security policy and the mail server, with the SMTP password and TOTP secrets encrypted at rest.
- File storage in Postgres: images re-encoded to WebP with a thumbnail and their metadata stripped, PDFs kept as uploaded, served with content-hash ETags.
- An optional nickname on the account page, with a switch to show it instead of the full name on recipes and everywhere else others see the person; administrators still see the full name.
- An optional avatar picture on the account page, shown wherever the person's name is (in the header, on recipes, in the household, on public recipes while the author shows their name there, and on paired instances under the same rule as the name) and deleted with the account.
- File permissions: a stored file opens only for its uploader, as an avatar or branding image, or as an image of a recipe the reader may see; an image goes on a recipe only when the editor may read it, and only the uploader deletes a file while nothing uses it.
- A nightly sweep deletes uploads that nothing has used for 30 days, such as the images of an editor draft that was discarded; images an old revision still names stay until the revision is purged.
- Recipes with step-by-step instructions where each step lists the ingredients it uses; the ingredient list, nutrition per serving and allergens are derived from the steps and scale with the servings. Drafts, publishing, tags, cover and step images, source attribution.
- A recipe's own ingredient list next to the steps: the editor's steps tab opens with a summary card where the cook adds ingredients directly, takes over ingredients only the steps name, and sees which ingredients, or how much of them, no step uses yet. The recipe's list, nutrition, allergens and shopping take the larger of the declared and the used amount, and the list travels with revisions, `.cui` files, paired instances and agent drafts.
- Recipe, ingredient and resource text in German and English, with a fallback to the language a recipe was written in and a translation per language in the editor; full text search in both languages.
- An ingredient catalogue with nutrition per 100 g, unit conversions, density and the 14 EU allergens, with an optional prefill from USDA FoodData Central.
- Units, tags and ingredient categories as shared resources, seeded in both languages.
- A food fact of the day as the first card of the start page: every base ingredient has short facts in German and English, drawn from Wikipedia and linked to the article and the ingredient, the same fact for everyone on a day.
- Favourites, ratings, private cook notes, and private allergies and intolerances that warn their owner on recipes and shopping lists.
- A cooking calendar of what everyone cooked, with "on this day" in earlier years; a personal meal plan; shared shopping lists that merge amounts, sort by aisle and update live on every open screen.
- Meal plan suggestions on large screens: recipes worth planning in the shown week, ranked from what the plan's household (or only you) cooked, how long ago and how regularly, favourites, the season, what was cooked around these dates in earlier years and the chosen meal, varied so similar dishes spread out, each with a short reason. Drag one onto a meal or plan it through the meal dialog.
- Diets as a kind of food restriction (vegetarian, vegan, pescetarian, halal, no pork, no beef, no alcohol), diet markers on ingredients, and the diets each recipe fits, with a diet filter on the recipe list.
- Food restrictions by ingredient name: an unknown name becomes a draft ingredient, so anyone can mark an intolerance to any food.
- Dinner guests: choose who is coming and see which recipes would not suit them, without learning who or why; only users who opt in to sharing with hosts count.
- Recipe visibility: private, household, shared with chosen people and groups, everyone on the instance, paired instances, or public; private stays private for administrators too. Admin privacy settings with a default visibility, optionally locked, a personal default per user, and a switch that allows public recipes (off by default; while off a public recipe is only visible to signed-in users). Two account settings decide whether the author's name is shown on public recipes and on paired instances.
- Households: invite and accept, a shared meal plan and default shopping list, members preselected as dinner guests. Members switch between the household's meal plan and their personal one, which only they see.
- Sub-recipes: a step may use another recipe, scaled by its yield or servings, counted through for nutrition, allergens, diets and warnings, and expanded on shopping lists unless you buy it ready-made.
- Deletion rules: accounts deleted without a trace with a handover of their recipes and lists, recipes with their images, ingredients merged or (when restrictions name them) archived invisibly, units merged, categories replaced; impact endpoints for every confirmation dialog.
- The base catalogue: units, categories, tags and about 390 ingredients with USDA nutrition, seeded once per key and never brought back after a deletion.
- Recipes as `.cui` files: anyone signed in exports a recipe they can open from its page, with its sub-recipes and visible variations; cooks import `.cui` files and older archives as drafts from the drafts list, and a recipe imported before can update the earlier import instead of adding a copy. Administrators keep the bulk export.
- The catalogue file: administrators export the whole catalogue as one `cuisine-catalogue` JSON file and import such a file from another instance after a preview, which creates new entries active and changes an existing entry only in the fields the administrator accepts, allergens and diet markers listed first.
- Peering, behind its own feature switch (on for a fresh instance): administrators pair two Cuisine instances once with a one-time invitation link, the handshake negotiates the peering protocol and format versions and exchanges a signing secret in each direction, and both admin areas list the paired instance with its versions, capabilities and per-peer switches; either side can refresh or revoke the pairing, and pairing, refusals, revokes and failed peer requests go to the security log.
- Peer channels carry events between paired instances: thin signed events with a sequence number per channel, delivered by a background worker that retries forever, an inbound queue, catch-up after a gap, full reconciliation after a source was restored from a backup, a catch-up every 15 minutes and a daily comparison and ping (which also serve an instance that cannot receive events), version announcements after an upgrade, and the channel status with "sync now" on the Peers tab. A page at `/peering/accept` explains where to paste an invitation link opened in a browser. The peering protocol messages have JSON Schemas next to the format schemas.
- Sharing recipes with paired instances: owners pick a paired instance as a share target, every recipe set to "Paired instances" or public goes to every peer the administrator lets users share with (also to a peer paired later, at once), and the other instance shows read-only mirrors after an administrator accepted each recipe and decided how its catalogue entries map (reused for later recipes, correctable, with held updates for new entries); allergens and diet markers on mirrors are the union of both catalogues, mirrors keep their source's variations, accept local variations and copies, go on shopping lists with their plain-text lines, are never exported as `.cui` files, and vanish when withdrawn or the pairing ends. The web app offers paired instances in the share dialog, shows mirrors with their source and a link to it, and gives administrators an incoming recipes queue with a decision dialog and a mappings view per peer.
- The visibility "Paired instances" (`peers`) between everyone on the instance and public: every signed-in user here and every paired instance read it. It is offered only while peering is on; while peering is off a stored one reads as everyone on the instance and its recipes leave the peers until peering is on again. Switching peering on or off publishes a new privacy statement version. Copies of mirrors can never be set to it.
- "Sync all from this instance", a per-peer switch on the receiving side: recipes from that peer become mirrors at once and updates apply without waiting, with automatic catalogue decisions (matched by name, otherwise created as drafts for the review queue; unknown units left out, tags dropped) saved as correctable mappings. Rejected recipes stay rejected. Switching it on offers once to accept everything that waits. A new pairing starts with it on.
- Fresh instances share by default: the default visibility is "Paired instances" (locked, as before) and peering is on. Existing instances keep their stored settings.
- Suggested by peers: catalogue values a paired instance sent that differ from the mapped local entry (another density, a missing conversion, more nutrition values, other names, other allergens) are listed in the Catalogue tab next to the reference data updates, warnings first, and accepted or dismissed per field; a dismissed suggestion comes back only when the instance sends another value.
- `make e2e-peering`, an on-demand end to end run of peering between two throwaway instances: pairing, sharing, updates, a held update, withdrawal, offline catch-up, a version mismatch and revoke.
- An MCP surface for AI agents to search recipes and import recipes from websites and PDFs as drafts, with per-token budgets and an agent call log.
- Hosted AI apps such as ChatGPT and claude.ai connect to the MCP surface through OAuth instead of a pasted token: Cuisine publishes the OAuth discovery documents, lets apps register themselves, and asks the signed-in person on a consent page what the app may do. Connections last as long as an agent token may, refresh tokens rotate and end the connection when reused, and each person sees and disconnects their connected apps on the AI agents tab. The agent settings switch OAuth connections off and can limit them to listed app hosts.
- New permissions: write and manage for recipes and ingredients, write:resources and use:mcp; integration and agent sections in the admin settings.
- Three storage providers for uploaded files, chosen with `STORAGE__PROVIDER`: Postgres (the default), a local volume or SeaweedFS over the S3 API with presigned URLs served read-only at `/objects/`, and a storage CLI that copies, verifies and removes files when switching.
- The admin area's readings: host (machine, database and Redis), and storage and backup (provider, sizes, the nightly dumps and warnings when files or backups share the database's machine or miss the backup).
- Branding and display defaults: accent colour, the sign-in background photo, unit system, date and time format and first day of the week.
- Notifications: webhooks with Standard Webhooks signatures, an event catalogue the cookbook adds to, a delivery log with automatic and manual retries and no trace of deleted users; an in-app banner with a schedule; in-app notices every user acknowledges once, with an overview of who did.
- Mail as glados has it: sender name, reply-to, certificate verification, an hourly limit and alert recipients; per-language HTML templates with packaged defaults, a sandboxed preview, and template file import and export; operational alerts for failed backups, webhooks switched off after repeated failures, maintenance mode and new storage warnings.
- Maintenance mode that makes the app read-only with a banner, switchable in the admin area and from the maintenance sidecar; retention settings for the security log, agent calls, webhook deliveries and recipe revisions with a nightly purge; "back up now" through the backup sidecar, with restore guidance; a log viewer of recent warnings and errors with secrets redacted.
- Feature switches in one place (AI agents, webhooks, households, dinner guests, USDA lookup).
- Versioned imprint and privacy statement per language, public for the login page; a default privacy statement written from the instance's configuration, naming the privacy mode, the backup retention and the deletion rules; the statement blocks every user until they acknowledge its current version, and declining signs them out.
- Voluntary consents for health data and AI agents with their times: agent tokens need the AI agents consent and withdrawing it revokes them; withdrawing the health data consent deletes the allergies, intolerances and diets.
- A groups directory every signed-in user can read, for sharing recipes with groups.
- A Docker Compose stack of prebuilt GHCR images with Postgres, Redis, nightly database dumps, a maintenance sidecar and an optional Cloudflare Tunnel; CI and a release workflow that smoke-tests the images on an empty database before it tags and publishes them on the merge of a release PR, and a weekly cleanup of old candidate images.
- Pickers that say more about what they offer: ingredients with their category, family, allergens and draft state as each picker needs, recipes with cover, time and author, people with their avatar, equipment with its icon, and units grouped by kind with what they weigh, including whether a recipe line's ingredient can be weighed in the chosen unit so its nutrition counts.
- Cuisine installs as an app on Android and iOS: a web app manifest under the instance's own name, language and accent colour, app icons, and a service worker that keeps the app shell on the device so the app opens quickly, while recipes and sessions always come fresh from the server.
- Release images for linux/arm64 next to linux/amd64 under the same tags, so Cuisine runs on a 64-bit Raspberry Pi; the release smoke test starts the images on both platforms.

### Fixed

- Small gram and litre amounts read exactly (0,125 l instead of 0,1 l); on the account page each person may choose kitchen fractions of a litre or kilogram instead (⅛ l, 1 ½ kg).
- Notes on the recipe's own ingredient list ("weich", "festkochend") show on the recipe page, the public page and for agents, and step notes show in the list when the list has none.
- A recipe that uses a draft ingredient claims no diet until a person activates it, and the dinner guest check counts a draft ingredient against every diet.
- Ingredient lines with a count of pieces or a weight take the plural ("6 Stk. Karotten", "450 g Karotten").
- An ingredient named like an ingredient family ("Essig", "Öl") joins that family instead of being refused, also from the recipe editor's quick dialog; a name conflict says which kind of entry holds the name.
- Ingredient search answers exact names and aliases first ("Salz" before "Kräutersalz", "Zucker" finds Kristallzucker first).
- Agents read in their user's language by default, and a repeated draft call with the same idempotency key answers exactly as the first one did.
- Agents are guided to put each ingredient on the step that uses it and to mention it in the step text: the draft tool shows a worked example, mentions may name a step's line by name (`@[Zwiebeln](ingredient)`), also for ingredients the same call creates, and the answer carries hints for the next draft.
- Agents are asked to add every language Cuisine keeps (de-AT and en-GB) to the recipes they draft, with a hint naming each language still missing, and may mention ingredients by name in a translated step too.
- Agents can name a draft ingredient in another language with the new MCP tool `add_ingredient_translation`, so ingredients a recipe draft created from a name no longer stay in one language; the draft's hints name each such ingredient with its id.
- The co-editors dialog offers its person picker again.
- OpenVue's own texts (empty lists, result counts, close buttons) speak German in the German UI, and the sign-in page uses the instance's default language.
- The unit picker shows small factors exactly ("= 0,001 g"), and "nach Geschmack" stays on one line.
- The base catalogue finds Hühnerhaxe as "Hühnerschenkel" and plain flour as "Mehl".
