## 1 languages

1. Content is written in `de-AT` or `en-GB` (`ContentLocale`). The UI languages are the same two; `translations.ui_locale` maps the UI codes `deAT` and `enGB` onto them.
2. Every translatable record keeps its text in a translation table of its own, keyed by parent and locale: units, tags, ingredient categories, ingredients, recipes, recipe steps and step ingredient notes. The domain holds them as `dict[locale, <Text>]` (`domain/value_objects/texts.py`).
3. Recipes and ingredients record their `original_locale`, and their text in it is required (`translations.assert_translations`). Other languages are optional.
4. A read resolves the requested locale, then the original, then any (`translations.resolve`). Responses carry the resolved text, the `locale` actually served and `available_locales`. A route takes `?locale=`; without it the viewer's UI language applies.
5. The editor reads and writes every language at once (`GET /recipes/{id}/edit`, `PUT /recipes/{id}`); `PUT /recipes/{id}/translation` adds or replaces one language by step order.
6. Search runs over every translation of a recipe and the names of its ingredients. On Postgres it is full text search in the `german` or `english` dictionary of each translation plus a substring match on the title; on SQLite a substring match stands in (`recipe_repository.py`).
7. A recipe's description, tips and step texts are a Markdown subset: `**bold**`, `*italic*`, bullet lists (`- `), numbered lists (`1. `), paragraphs and line breaks. The frontend's editor writes nothing else and its renderer shows nothing else, with HTML escaped; older plain text is valid as it is, since a single line break renders as a break. The backend stores the text as it arrives and never renders it. Postgres full text search drops the marks as punctuation, and a substring match still finds the words inside them; where the backend hands out plain text it strips the marks (`markdown.plain_text`), such as the summaries' `description_text` for cards and snippets. MCP's `get_recipe` returns the Markdown as it is, and `create_recipe_draft` documents that its text fields take the subset. Titles, ingredient notes and all other names stay plain text. Step text may also mention one of its own lines as `@[Name](ingredient:<id>)` or `@[Name](recipe:<id>)`; the stored name is only a fallback, since the frontend shows the line's current name.

## 2 permissions

1. Every signed-in user reads the published recipes their visibility opens to them ([section 6b](#6b-recipe-visibility)), the ingredient catalogue, the resources and the cooking calendar of those recipes.
2. `write:recipes` creates recipes and edits, publishes, unpublishes and deletes the actor's own; `manage:recipes` does so for every recipe the manager can see, never for a draft or a private recipe of someone else (`recipe_rules.py`). Co-editors with `write:recipes` edit the content of a recipe they can see, but neither publish, unpublish, delete, change its visibility nor its co-editors (`can_edit_recipe` against `can_delete_recipe`, [section 3f](#3f-variations-co-editors-and-copies)). Adding a variation needs `write:recipes` and a recipe the actor can see.
3. `write:ingredients` creates ingredients and edits the actor's own; `manage:ingredients` edits, activates and deletes everybody's.
4. `write:resources` changes units, tags and ingredient categories. Exporting and importing the catalogue file needs `write:resources` or `manage:ingredients` with `write:ingredients`, per kind ([section 4d](#4d-the-catalogue-file)).
5. Favourites, ratings, notes, food restrictions, cook events, the meal plan and shopping lists need only a session, because each user works on their own records (or on lists shared with them).
6. A recipe the viewer may not see (a draft, a private recipe, one not shared with them) answers 404, like an unknown one, so its existence never leaks.

## 3 recipes and what is derived

1. A recipe holds two ingredient lists that overlap: the lines of its steps and the ingredient list the cook declares for the whole recipe (`recipe_ingredients`, `ingredients` on `RecipeRequest` and `RecipeEditResponse`, lines in the shape of a step's lines: an ingredient or a sub-recipe, amount, unit, notes, with public ids kept like those of step lines). The declared list may name what no step uses yet. The recipe's ingredient list, the nutrition, the allergens, the sub-recipe needs and the shopping lines are derived on every read from both (`recipe_derivation.derive`, through `merged_lines`). A merged line carries the notes of the declared lines of its ingredient when the cook wrote any there, distinct notes joined with a comma, otherwise a step note only when every step line of the ingredient has that same note ("gehackt"), so a note of one step ("zum Wälzen", "der Rest") never reads as the note of the whole amount (`merged_notes`); an ingredient split over several units carries them on its first line. A line's name takes the plural for a bare count, a count of pieces and any weight or volume ("6 Stk. Karotten", "450 g Karotten"), and the singular for other units ("2 Zehen Knoblauch"); uncountable ingredients carry the same plural as singular (`presenters.name_amount`).
2. `merged_lines` is the one merge. Per ingredient the step lines and the declared lines are each summed per unit, and the larger side counts: in that unit when both use one and the same, in grams when every unit involved can be weighed, and otherwise per group of comparable units (one kind with a factor to its base unit, or a single unit), where the side with more in the base unit keeps its lines. So a step line is never lost and nothing read from the list undercounts. Declared lines come first in their order, then the ingredients only the steps name. Without a declared list, the step lines of one unit add up, lines of several units add up in grams when every one of them can be weighed and stay separate otherwise, exactly as before. A "to taste" line (no amount) appears only when neither side measures the ingredient. Per sub-recipe the side needing more batches keeps its lines. The editor's summary card warns per declared line when no step uses it or the steps use less, compared the same way, and says when it cannot be compared.
3. Amounts scale by `servings / base_servings`; the detail route takes `?servings=`, and the step ingredients are scaled the same way.
4. Nutrition sums every measured step ingredient that can be weighed and has nutrition. `complete` is false as soon as one could not be counted, and `missing_ingredient_ids` names the ingredients without nutrition. Per serving divides the recipe's own total by `base_servings`.
5. A recipe is created as a draft. Publishing needs at least one step, the original title and every step's text in the original language (`validate_publishable`).
6. Saving a recipe replaces its steps and its declared list as a whole. Unknown tag, ingredient, unit or image ids answer 404 and nothing is saved.
6a. Steps, ingredient lines and equipment lines have public ids of their own (`id` in the editor and detail responses, `line_id` on a step's ingredient in the detail). A save keeps the id a step or line of the request names when the recipe has that row now, so links keep pointing at it; a new row, a repeated id and an id the recipe does not have (another recipe's, another instance's, a made-up one) get a fresh id (`step_ids.StepIdKeepers`). Creating a recipe, importing one and "Copy as my recipe" always give fresh ids. The editor sends back the ids it loaded.
6b. The step image library offers photos that steps already show for another step, since many are interchangeable (chopped onions, a hot pan). `GET /step-images` (`write:recipes`, `ReadRecipes.step_images`) lists one entry per stored file that a step of a recipe the caller may see shows, mirrors left out because their photos belong to another instance's cooks. It takes the `ingredient_id` and `equipment_id` values of the step being edited (unknown ids are ignored, since they only rank) and an optional `search` over step text, recipe titles and the steps' ingredient names. Entries sharing the most of those ingredients and equipment come first (`matches`), then the caller's own, then the most recently changed; each names the recipe, the step number and the step's plain text through the step that fits best. Picking one puts the same file on the step under the rule of [file-storage.md](file-storage.md) section 1 point 6, and deleting either recipe keeps the file while the other uses it ([section 6d](#6d-deletion) item 4). The editor opens the library from a step's photo field (`StepImageLibraryDialog`).

## 3a sub-recipes

1. A step ingredient names a catalogue ingredient or another recipe (`sub_recipe_id`), exactly one, with amount, unit and note as usual. A recipe may state a yield (`yield_amount` with `yield_unit_id`, such as 500 g).
2. How much of a sub-recipe a line needs (`sub_recipes.sub_recipe_factor`): in the sub-recipe's yield unit, amount / yield batches; otherwise the amount counts servings of the sub-recipe; without an amount, one batch.
3. Nutrition, allergens, diets, the viewer's warnings and the dinner guest check go through every sub-recipe, scaled, whatever the viewer may see of them (`sub_recipes.leaves`, `LookupLoader.load_for`). The recipe's own `ingredients` list leaves sub-recipes out; the detail lists them under `needs` with the scaled amount, the batches and the sub-recipe's own total time.
4. A sub-recipe the viewer may not see appears as a placeholder: `sub_recipe` with `id` and `title` null and `accessible` false, and an empty name. Linking needs the sub-recipe to be visible to the editor. The editor response lists `less_visible_sub_recipe_ids`, the direct sub-recipes some reader of the recipe may not see (drafts, narrower visibility), which publishing should warn about (`recipe_rules.narrower`). For a recipe that is public in effect these are exactly the sub-recipes that are not public themselves, so the editor names them as the ones visitors without an account cannot read, both when a draft is published and when a published recipe is made public (which it offers to take back).
5. No cycles, and at most three levels below the top recipe, counted across the whole tree the recipe belongs to (the recipes using it as well as those it uses); both are refused with 422 (`SubRecipeCycleError`, `SubRecipeTooDeepError`). A recipe another recipe uses cannot be deleted (409 `RecipeInUseError`).
6. Shopping and the meal plan expand the tree into catalogue ingredients. Each item records every recipe it is for (`recipe_id` for the first, `extra_recipe_ids` for those whose amounts joined it later) and its `sub_recipe_path`, outermost first ("for Lasagna sheets, in Lasagna"); items merge only with items of the same ingredient, unit and path. `bought_sub_recipe_ids` on `POST /shopping-lists/{id}/recipes` ("I'll buy it") adds a direct sub-recipe as one item (no ingredient, no text, the sub-recipe as the last path entry, named from it when visible) instead of its ingredients. `POST /meal-plan/shopping-list` takes the same `bought_sub_recipe_ids` for the whole range: each applies to every planned recipe that uses it directly, and one no planned recipe uses directly answers 404.
7. An agent links a sub-recipe through `sub_recipe_id` in `create_recipe_draft`.

## 3b ingredient mentions

1. Step text may mention an ingredient, sub-recipe or equipment item as `@[Name](ingredient:<public id>)`, `@[Name](recipe:<public id>)` or `@[Name](equipment:<public id>)` (`mentions.py`). On every save and translation each mention must point at a line or item of its own step, or the save answers 422 `InvalidMentionError` naming the step, locale and mention ([section 4c](#4c-equipment-and-the-kitchen)).
2. Plain-text outputs reduce a mention to its name (`markdown.plain_text`, used by `description_text` and anything the backend hands out as plain text). MCP `create_recipe_draft` documents and accepts mentions with the ids of the step's own lines.

## 3c recipe revisions

1. Every use case that changes a recipe's content appends a revision in the same unit of work (`RevisionRecorder`): create, update, translation, an agent's image (`image_attached`) and restore, drafts included. Publishing and unpublishing change the status only and record nothing. Quick successive saves are not merged.
2. A revision (`recipe_revisions`) has a number per recipe (1, 2, 3), its kind (`created`, `updated`, `translated`, `image_attached`, `restored`, `adopted`), when and how (`web` or `mcp`) it was made, the author (null once the account is gone), the revision a restore went back to and an optional note (`revision_note` on `RecipeRequest`). An `adopted` revision also names the variation author it credits (`credited_author_id`, `credited_author` in the summary; null once that account is gone). It is never changed and goes with its recipe.
3. The snapshot (`revisions.build_snapshot`) is the editable recipe in the shape of `RecipeEditResponse`, by public id: metadata with the yield, every translation, the steps with their own ids and text (mentions included), ingredient and sub-recipe lines with their ids and notes, step equipment with ids and notes, tag ids and image ids, and the declared ingredient list under `ingredients` (snapshots written before it existed lack the key and restore without one). Snapshots written before steps and lines had ids lack them; staleness ignores the ids when comparing lines. `names` records the display names per locale of every referenced ingredient, unit, tag, equipment item and sub-recipe at that moment, so old revisions stay readable after renames and deletions. Status, visibility, shares, the personal layer, cook events and derived data are never in it. `schema_version` lets a later shape upgrade older snapshots on read (`upgrade_snapshot`).
4. Whoever may edit the recipe reads its history (`GET /recipes/{id}/revisions`, newest first; `GET /recipes/{id}/revisions/{number}` with the snapshot); readers of a published recipe do not.
5. `POST /recipes/{id}/revisions/{number}/restore` saves the snapshot's content as the recipe, through the same checks as any save, and appends a `restored` revision; history is never rewritten and the status, visibility and shares stay. The steps and lines get back the ids the snapshot recorded. Revisions keep no stored file alive: a vanished image is dropped. A vanished ingredient, unit, tag, equipment item or sub-recipe fails the restore with 409 `RevisionRestoreError`, naming it by kind and recorded name (`missing_kind`, `missing_name`).
6. Agents have no revision tool; their changes are recorded with `created_via` mcp. The frontend computes field diffs from two snapshots.
7. The nightly retention purge deletes revisions older than `recipe_revisions_days` of the retention settings (0 keeps them forever), always keeping each recipe's newest revision (`RevisionPurger`, registered by `bootstrap/cookbook_hooks.py`).

## 3d webhook events

1. Publishing a recipe announces `recipe.published` with `{"recipe": {"id", "title", "description", "locale", "visibility"}}` in the original language, as plain text without Markdown marks or mentions (`markdown.plain_text`). A private recipe is never announced.
2. Deleting a recipe that was ever published announces `recipe.deleted` with `{"recipe": {"id"}}`, also when an account deletion removes it. Drafts that never went out are not announced.
3. The cookbook publishes through `IEventPublisher` (`ports/outbound/event_publisher.py`), which Platform's webhook publisher fulfils inside the use case's transaction; the composition root registers both events in Platform's catalogue (`bootstrap/webhook_events.py`).

## 3e recipe export and import (`.cui`)

1. A `.cui` file is a zip archive with media type `application/vnd.cuisine.recipe+zip` (`CUI_MEDIA_TYPE` in `recipe_archive_router.py`): `recipes.json`, a `cuisine-recipes` document whose shape, versions and compatibility rules are in [formats.md](formats.md), and the images' original bytes under `files/`. Each recipe carries its content (built by the format mapper, not the revision snapshot), title, status, source public id and the variations the exporter may see; sub-recipes the exporter may see are added and come before the recipes using them, and one the exporter may not see keeps no title. A `catalogue` holds every unit, tag, ingredient and equipment item the recipes and their variations use. Ratings, notes, favourites and cook events never go into a file.
2. `GET /recipes/{id}/export` answers one recipe as a `.cui` file for anyone signed in who can open it: reading is a session plus the recipe's visibility, and so is exporting, with no permission beyond that. A mirror of a recipe from a paired instance is the exception: it answers 409 `MirrorReadOnlyError` ([section 6f](#6f-sharing-with-paired-instances-and-mirrors) item 21). A recipe the caller may not see, a draft of someone else's included, answers 404 like an unknown one. The response is `Content-Disposition: attachment` with the slug of the title as the name (`<slug>.cui`, lower case ASCII letters, digits and hyphens, `recipe.cui` when nothing is left), both filename forms, `X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff` and `Cache-Control: no-store`. Anonymous visitors have no export route; the public surface ([section 6e](#6e-public-recipes)) offers no download.
3. `GET /admin/recipes/export?recipe_id=` (manage:recipes; repeatable, every recipe the caller may see without, mirrors always left out) is the administrator's bulk export: the same file for many recipes, named `cuisine-recipes-<date>.cui`, with the same headers. The admin area offers it next to the import.
4. `POST /recipes/import/preview` and `POST /recipes/import` (write:recipes; multipart `file`, at most 512 MB) read `.cui` files and the zip archives of earlier releases alike, through one use case (`RecipeArchive`); the admin area uses the same routes. The preview answers each recipe of the file with `earlier`, the caller's own recipe from an earlier import of it (id, title, status) or null, and stores nothing. The import takes the source ids whose earlier import the caller wants updated as repeated `update` form fields.
5. The import matches every catalogue entry to this instance: by seed key, then by name or alias in any language (units by code). An unmatched ingredient is created as a draft owned by the importer, an unmatched equipment item as a draft item, both for the review queue. An unmatched unit leaves its amounts without a unit, an unmatched tag is dropped, a sub-recipe that is not in the file drops its line, an unreadable image is left out, and a new recipe whose title a recipe the importer can see already has is imported anyway; each of these is reported in `conflicts` with its kind and name. Mentions are rewritten to the matched ids, or become plain text. Images are stored as uploads are (re-encoded, with thumbnails).
6. Every recipe the import creates becomes a draft of the importer with a `created` revision noted "Imported", saved through the same checks as any save, all in one transaction: a file that is not a zip, lacks `recipes.json`, has another format, a newer version or a malformed document answers 422 `RecipeArchiveError` and imports nothing. Older format versions are read through the upgrade chain ([formats.md section 5](formats.md#5-compatibility-rules)).
7. Origin: a created recipe stores where it came from, `origin_instance_id` (the document's `instance_id`) and `origin_public_id` (the recipe's `id` in the file). Files of the first release name no instance, so their recipes get no origin. `uq_recipes_origin` covers the owner and both columns, so every cook holds at most one recipe per origin and two cooks may each hold their own import of the same recipe. Handing a recipe over to another owner, or to none, clears its origin, since the new owner never imported it.
8. Updating the earlier import: a recipe whose source id is among `update` and whose origin matches a recipe the importer owns and may edit is saved into that recipe instead, as a normal `updated` revision noted "Updated from import", through the same checks as any save. The recipe keeps its visibility, shares, co-editors and status; a published recipe whose new content may not be published (it has no steps) goes back to draft and the change is reported with the kind `status`. A recipe imported before but not named in `update` is imported as a new copy, a new draft without an origin, so the earlier import stays the one later imports update. Each report entry says whether it was `updated`.
9. Step, line and variation ids: the save turns a source step, ingredient line, equipment line or declared line id into `uuid5(<local recipe public id>, "<kind>:<source id>")` (`domain/services/step_ids.py`), and an imported variation's id into `uuid5(<local recipe public id>, "variation:<source id>")`. An update of the earlier import therefore keeps the public id of every step and line the source still has, and replaces the variation the earlier import brought instead of adding it twice, as long as the importer is still its author (otherwise it is left as it is and reported with the kind `variation`). A new copy is another recipe and gets other ids. No mapping table is needed.
10. Each variation in the file is attached to the recipe as a variation of the importer, keeping its name, locale and private flag, with "Originally by <display name>" (or "another cook" when the file carries no name) added to its note. Its changes go through the same matching; a change naming an ingredient or sub-recipe without a match, or no longer fitting the recipe, is left out and reported in `conflicts` with the kind of that record; any other change that does not fit (a step text or an added step whose step the recipe no longer has) and a change of an unknown kind are reported with the kind `variation` and the variation's name.

## 3f variations, co-editors and copies

1. A variation (`recipe_variations`, `domain/entities/variation.py`) belongs to one recipe and stores only differences: a name, a short note, the content locale it is written in (no translations), a `private` flag, the recipe revision its step numbers refer to (`based_on_revision`) and its changes. A change names a step by its number from 1 in the recipe as it was at that revision, not by the step's public id.
2. A change has a stable `id` and a `kind` (`VariationChangeKind`, `domain/services/variations.py`): `swap` (another ingredient instead of one, in one step or, with no step, in every step of the original and in its declared ingredient list; it keeps each line's amount, unit and note unless `keep_amount` is false, and rewrites mentions of the swapped ingredient in that step's text), `add_line`, `remove_line` (the lines of a step naming an ingredient or a sub-recipe), `step_text` and `extra_step` (a new step after a step, 0 for first, with its own lines and equipment). Changes name records by public id in JSON, so a variation outlives a merged or deleted ingredient: such a change is reported as not `available` and left out. A save checks the changes against the recipe as it is now (422 `InvalidVariationError` naming the change; 422 `InvalidMentionError` for a mention outside its step, as in the editor) and moves the variation onto the recipe's latest revision. At most 100 changes.
3. Visibility: a variation is visible to whoever sees its recipe, never more; a private variation is its author's alone, administrators and `manage:recipes` included (`can_view_variation`). A hidden variation answers 404 like an unknown one. Creating needs `write:recipes` and a visible recipe (in instance mode every cook on every recipe); only the author changes it; the author or `manage:recipes` deletes it. The recipe's owner as such does not.
4. Derived: `GET /recipes/{id}/variations/{variation_id}/view` applies the variation (`apply_variation`) and derives everything from the result exactly as for a recipe: ingredient list for the wanted servings, nutrition with the extended nutrients, allergens, diets, the viewer's warnings, the dinner-guest flag and the missing kitchen equipment. The response is the recipe detail with the variation's rating, plus a marker per step (`origin_step`, `added`, `text_changed`, swapped and added lines by index, removed lines). A change whose step or line no longer exists is skipped (`applies` false), never applied elsewhere.
5. Staleness: each variation response compares the snapshot of its base revision with the recipe now for every step a change names; a step whose text or lines differ is `changed`, a step the recipe lost is `deleted`, each with the old text (`stale_steps`, `is_stale`). Without the base revision (none recorded yet, or purged by retention) `base_revision_available` is false and nothing is flagged.
6. Browse: summaries count the visible variations (`variation_count`). With `diets`, a recipe that misses a wanted diet is still listed when a visible variation fits them all (`variation_fits_diets`); with `guests`, a recipe that conflicts with a guest carries `variation_fits_guests` when a visible variation does not, and `hide_unsuitable_for_guests` keeps it ([section 6a](#6a-diets-and-dinner-guests)).
7. Adopting: the recipe's editors (owner, co-editors, `manage:recipes`) who can see a variation adopt all its changes or chosen ones (`POST /recipes/{id}/variations/{variation_id}/adopt {change_ids | null, revision_note}`). The original is saved with them as an `adopted` revision noted with the variation's name and crediting its author; the adopted changes leave the variation, the remaining step numbers move past the steps the adopted changes added, and the variation moves onto the new revision. A variation whose changes were all adopted stays, empty, until its author or a manager deletes it.
8. Ratings and cook events may name a visible variation (`variation_id`). Each user has one rating for the recipe itself and one per variation (a partial unique index for the first). The recipe's `rating_average` and `rating_count` stay its own; the detail adds `rating_overall_average` and `rating_overall_count` over the recipe and all its variations, and each variation carries its own average, count and the viewer's rating. Deleting a variation deletes its ratings and clears it from cook events.
9. Co-editors (`recipe_co_editors`): the owner alone sets them (`PUT /recipes/{id}/co-editors {user_ids}`, 403 for anyone else); each must be an active person other than the owner who can see the recipe (422 `CoEditorError`). They edit the content like the owner, revisions record them as authors, and they count as editors for the history and for adopting. They cannot delete, publish or unpublish, change the visibility or shares, or change the co-editors. Narrowing the visibility takes their rights away with their access.
10. Copy as my recipe: `POST /recipes/{id}/copy` (`write:recipes`, a visible recipe) creates an independent draft of the actor's with the same content, images and sub-recipe lines, the actor's default visibility and a `created` revision, linked by `recipes.based_on_recipe_id`. The copy shows `based_on` (the original with title and owner when the viewer may see it, an anonymous placeholder otherwise); the original shows `based_on_count` and `GET /recipes/{id}/copies`, both counting only copies the viewer may see (so a draft copy counts for its owner alone). Deleting the original clears the link.
11. Agents read variations through `get_recipe` with `include_variations`; there are no variation tools.

## 3g printing

1. A signed-in reader prints a recipe from its page, with the Print action or the browser's own print, and saves a PDF through the browser. There is no server-side PDF.
2. The paper shows what the page shows: the chosen servings, the chosen content language and the chosen variation, named under the title. It carries the title, description, times, difficulty, season, diets, author and co-editors, the cover, the needed sub-recipes, the scaled ingredient list, equipment, the steps with their own ingredients, nutrition per serving, allergens, tips and the source.
3. It leaves out the page's controls, the variation switcher and its markings, the reader's warnings and dinner guest notes, ratings, the reader's note and the cooking history.
4. A QR code at the foot links to `<origin>/r/<public id>`, the recipe's or, while a variation is shown, the variation's, which opens it through the resolver after signing in. The code is drawn in the browser, never by an outside service.
5. Public pages never print a recipe ([section 6e](#6e-public-recipes)).

## 4 ingredients and units

1. A unit has a stable `code`, a kind and, for mass and volume, a factor to grams or millilitres. Count units (piece, clove, slice) weigh nothing by themselves.
2. Amount to grams (`quantity.to_grams`): the ingredient's own conversion for the unit wins (one egg is 55 g, one cup of flour what it weighs); then mass by factor; then volume by factor times the ingredient's density. Anything else cannot be weighed.
3. Allergens are the 14 EU allergens (`Allergen`); a recipe's allergens are the union of its ingredients'.
4. Diet markers (`DietMarker`: meat, poultry, pork, beef, fish, seafood, gelatin, dairy, eggs, honey, alcohol) say what an ingredient is or contains as far as diets care. An unmarked ingredient fits every diet, so markers matter as much as allergens. A draft ingredient fits none until a person activates it (`diet_rules.unconfirmed`), since an agent may have created it from a name alone.
4. An ingredient an agent created starts as a draft (`IngredientStatus.DRAFT`); it is usable in recipes at once and is activated by a person.
5. A unit used by a step ingredient or a conversion cannot be deleted; an ingredient used by a recipe cannot be deleted. A deleted tag leaves its recipes; a deleted category leaves its ingredients uncategorised.

## 4a families, aliases and unique names

1. An ingredient family (`/ingredient-families`, write:resources) groups variants of one food, such as fresh, canned and puréed tomatoes. An ingredient belongs to at most one family (`family_id`) and keeps its own nutrition, allergens, markers and conversions; deleting a family leaves its ingredients without one. The ingredient list filters by `family_id`; shopping items carry `family_id`, so variants sit next to each other without merging.
2. A food restriction may target a family (`family_id`), covering every member, also later ones; the privacy rules are those of every restriction.
3. Aliases are other names per locale next to the main name (`aliases`), used by search, pickers, typed restriction names and agent name matching; display uses the main name. Merging moves the source's names and aliases into the target's aliases.
4. Ingredient and family main names and aliases form one namespace per locale, compared without case, diacritics or extra spaces (`names.name_key`); tags are unique per locale, unit codes and unit names likewise. A taken name answers 409 `DuplicateNameError` with `existing_id`, `existing_name` and `existing_kind` (ingredient, family, equipment, unit or tag), so a client knows which catalogue the id belongs to. A family may share a name with its own members: an ingredient saved without a family whose name is a family's name in one of its languages ("Essig", "Öl") joins that family, and one in another family answers 409 naming the family.
5. `GET /ingredients?search=` matches main names and aliases in every language; an item found through an alias only carries it as `matched_alias`, so a picker can show "Tomate → Paradeiser". Matches come best first (`names.search_rank`): a name, plural or alias equal to the search, then names holding it as a whole word ("schwarzer Pfeffer"), names starting with it ("Pfefferoni"), names with a word starting with it, and any other match ("Kräutersalz"); equally good matches stay in alphabetical order. MCP `search_ingredients` answers in the same order. `GET /ingredients/duplicates?name=&locale=` lists ingredients that probably mean the same food: exact (the same name apart from case, diacritics and spacing, which creating refuses) or near (one plural ending less, German -n/-en/-e/-s and umlauts, English -s/-es, or one letter apart on names of four to eight letters, two on longer ones), each with the matched name, for "Did you mean Erdapfel?".
6. A draft ingredient created from a typed restriction name has no owner and nothing else that tells its origin; it reads like any ownerless draft.
7. Seed: `families.yaml` (key, names), and per ingredient in `ingredients.yaml` an optional `family: <key>` and `aliases: {en-GB: [...], de-AT: [...]}`, with the seed-key rules of section 9. Families are seeded together with the base ingredients. `test_reference_data.py` checks the namespace rule on the seed.

## 4b extended nutrients and seasons

1. Next to the eight label values an ingredient may state extended nutrients per 100 g: cholesterol, caffeine, vitamins A (RAE), B1, B2, B6, B12, folate (DFE), C, D, E and K, calcium, iron, magnesium, phosphorus, potassium, zinc, iodine, and mono- and polyunsaturated and trans fat. They are stored as a map (`extended_nutrition`) from a stable key that names the unit, such as `vitamin_c_mg`, to the amount. The catalogue of keys with unit, label key and USDA nutrient id is `domain/value_objects/nutrients.py`, served by `GET /nutrition/nutrients`; any other key answers 422. A missing key means unknown, never zero.
2. The USDA lookup returns the extended values it finds (`extended_nutrition` on the food match), converting vitamin D from international units and computing folate as dietary folate equivalents; the build script fills them for the base catalogue, and MCP `create_ingredient` accepts them.
3. The recipe detail sums every extended nutrient at least one ingredient states (`nutrition.extended`, sub-recipes included), for the asked servings and per serving. A value is `complete` only when every measured amount could be weighed and every counted ingredient states that nutrient.
4. An ingredient may list the months (1 to 12) in which it is in season in Austria (`season_months`); an empty list means not seasonal. A recipe's `season_months` are the months in which every seasonal ingredient it uses, through its sub-recipes, is in season; it is empty when it uses none. `in_season` says whether the current month (UTC) is one of them. The list takes `in_season=true`, which filters in memory like the diets ([section 6a](#6a-diets-and-dinner-guests)), and `GET /home` has an `in_season` section.

## 4c equipment and the kitchen

1. The equipment catalogue (`/equipment`) holds cookware, bakeware, appliances and utensils (`EquipmentKind`) with names and aliases per locale, an optional `icon` (an @mdi/font name without the prefix), a `basic` flag for everyday items and a `seed_key`. Every signed-in user reads it; `write:resources` creates, edits, activates, merges and deletes items.
2. Equipment names and aliases share the namespace of ingredient and family names (section 4a): a name means one thing in a locale, and a taken one answers 409 `DuplicateNameError`. `GET /equipment/duplicates` applies the near-duplicate rules of `GET /ingredients/duplicates`.
3. Seeded items are never deleted or merged away (409 `SeededResourceError`). An item a recipe step names is blocked (409 `EquipmentInUseError`); `GET /equipment/{id}/deletion-impact` gives the count and the recipes the viewer can see, and `POST /equipment/{id}/merge {into_id}` moves steps and kitchens to the target, keeps the source's names as the target's aliases and deletes the source.
4. A step lists its equipment (`equipment[{equipment_id, count, notes}]`): an optional count such as two bowls and a translatable note such as "26 cm". Step text may mention one of the step's own items as `@[Name](equipment:<public id>)`, validated like ingredient mentions; plain-text outputs reduce it to the name.
5. A mention that names nothing of its step answers 422 `InvalidMentionError` with `step` (from 1), `locale`, `mention_kind` and `mention_id` next to the message, for ingredient, recipe and equipment mentions alike.
6. The detail's `equipment` is "You'll need" (`equipment_rules.needed_equipment`): the union over the steps with the highest count a single step names, the recipe's own items first, then each sub-recipe's, marked with that `sub_recipe`.
7. An agent's `create_recipe_draft` names step equipment by id or by name or alias; an unknown name becomes a draft item (`EquipmentStatus.DRAFT`) that a person with `write:resources` activates.
8. The kitchen is the equipment a cook owns (`GET /kitchen`, `POST /kitchen/setup`, `PUT /kitchen`, `DELETE /kitchen`). In a household it is the household's and every member reads and changes it; without one it is personal, and a personal kitchen stays unused while its cook is in a household. Setting it up without a list ticks the basic items.
9. Until the kitchen is set up nothing is flagged: summaries and the detail carry `missing_equipment` and `kitchen_ready` as null, and the list ignores `only_kitchen_ready`. Afterwards they name the items of the whole tree, sub-recipes included, that the kitchen lacks, and `only_kitchen_ready=true` filters in memory. The kitchen is never shown to anyone outside its household and never enters the dinner guest check.
10. Seed: `equipment.yaml` (key, kind, names, aliases, optional icon and `basic`), with the seed-key rules of section 9. `test_reference_data.py` checks the shared namespace on the seed.

## 4d the catalogue file

1. The catalogue file is one `cuisine-catalogue` JSON document ([formats.md section 4](formats.md#4-the-cuisine-catalogue-document)) with every unit, tag, ingredient category and family, ingredient and equipment item, each keyed by its public id at the source. It carries no images. The use case is `CatalogueFile` (`application/use_cases/resources/catalogue_file.py`), the routes are in `catalogue_file_router.py` and the admin area offers both steps in the Catalogue tab.
2. `GET /admin/catalogue/export` answers the file as `cuisine-catalogue-<date>.json` (`application/json`, `Content-Disposition: attachment` with both filename forms, `X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff`, `Cache-Control: no-store`). It needs `manage:ingredients` or `write:resources` and always holds the whole catalogue, since every signed-in user reads it anyway: every unit, tag, category and family and the active ingredients and equipment. Drafts wait for review and archived ingredients have left the catalogue, so neither is exported.
3. Importing has two steps with the same multipart `file` (at most 32 MB and 50,000 entries). `POST /admin/catalogue/import/preview` writes nothing and answers, per kind, how many entries are new, how many were found here and how many of those differ, every new entry by name, every found entry with differences with the list of differing fields (`field`, `current`, `incoming`), and the conflicts already known, such as allergens, diet markers or kinds this instance does not know, which the import leaves out. `POST /admin/catalogue/import` takes the differences to write as repeated `accept` form fields, each `<kind>:<source id>:<field>`, and reports what it created, what it changed (with the fields), the kinds it left out and what it could not write.
4. Matching is the matching of the recipe import (`application/services/catalogue_matching.py`, also used by `RecipeArchive`): seed key first, then units by code, tags, categories and families by a name in any language, and ingredients and equipment by a name or alias in any language. The source's public id is never taken over.
5. Field names and values are those of the reference data updates ([section 9](#9-seeds) item 2a): names per locale, other names per locale, category, family, each nutrition value and extended nutrient, density, conversions (as unit code to grams), allergens, diet markers, season months, unit factor and kind, tag kind and colour, and equipment kind, icon and basic flag. References are compared by the record they point at and shown by name. A field the file leaves empty (a missing name, alias list, nutrient, reference, icon or colour) is a gap and never listed as a difference; empty allergens, diet markers and season months are statements and are compared. Entries with allergen or diet marker differences come first, and within an entry those two fields come first, as in the reference updates view.
6. A found entry is never overwritten silently: only the accepted fields are written, through the same checks as an edit, and every other field stays. An accepted field that is no longer a difference is ignored. A name or alias another catalogue entry holds ([section 4a](#4a-families-aliases-and-unique-names)) is reported and not written, and the entry's other accepted fields are still written.
7. Every new entry is created active, since an administrator imports a catalogue on purpose; the recipe import's drafts for the review queue do not apply. References inside the file are kept: an ingredient's category and family point at the entry their source id names in the same file (found or created), or else at a record found by seed key or name; its conversions point at the file's units, found or created. Kinds are written in the order categories, families, units, tags, ingredients, equipment. A new entry whose name is taken, or that breaks a rule, is reported and not created; an ingredient whose other names are taken is created without them.
8. Permissions per kind: ingredients need `manage:ingredients` and `write:ingredients`; units, tags, categories, families and equipment need `write:resources`. A caller who holds only one of the two sets gets the other kinds counted in the preview, marked `skipped`, and left out of the import (reported in `skipped`); a caller who holds neither gets 403.
9. The import runs in one transaction. A file that is no JSON, names another format, has a newer version or is malformed answers 422 `CatalogueFileError` and writes nothing; so does an `accept` value that names no kind of the file.
10. The field computation, the comparison and the writing of accepted values live in `application/services/catalogue_fields.py` (`current_fields`, `find_differences`, `CatalogueWriter`), shared with the suggestions from paired instances ([section 4e](#4e-suggestions-from-paired-instances)).

## 4e suggestions from paired instances

1. Each catalogue mapping of a paired instance that points at a local unit, tag, ingredient or equipment item keeps the latest catalogue entry the peer sent with a recipe ([section 6f](#6f-sharing-with-paired-instances-and-mirrors) item 8). Where the peer's values differ from the local entry, they are offered to an administrator as suggestions; the local catalogue never changes without an accepted one. The use case is `PeerSuggestions` (`application/use_cases/mirrors/peer_suggestions.py`), the routes are in `mirrors_router.py`, and the admin area shows them as "Suggested by peers" in the Catalogue tab, next to the reference data updates. Categories and families have no mappings and get no suggestions.
2. Field names, values and the comparison are those of the catalogue file ([section 4d](#4d-the-catalogue-file) items 5 and 10): an empty value from the peer is a gap and never listed, except allergens, diet markers and season months. Other names per locale and conversions are compared as what the peer adds: the suggested value is the local list joined with the peer's, or the local conversions with the peer's added or changed, so accepting never removes anything the peer left out. A conversion names its unit through the peer's unit mapping (a unit only the conversions name gets one too, see [section 6f](#6f-sharing-with-paired-instances-and-mirrors) item 6), or, for mirrors received before such mappings were recorded, through the unit's seed key as read from the documents of the peer's mirrors; a unit found neither way is left out and named in the suggestion's `unmapped_units`. An ingredient's category and family are found by seed key or name.
3. `GET /admin/peer-suggestions?peer_id=&kind=` (`read:settings`, 404 while peering is off, 404 `PeerNotFoundError` for an unknown peer) answers the suggestions grouped by local entry (`kind`, `id`, `name`, `warning`, `suggestions` with `peer`, `source_id`, `field`, `current`, `suggested`, `unmapped_units`). Entries with an allergen or diet marker suggestion come first and carry `warning`; within an entry those two fields come first. Archived ingredients are left out.
4. `POST /admin/peer-suggestions/accept {peer_id, kind, source_id, field}` writes the peer's value of that one field through the normal use cases, so every rule of an edit applies: a name or other name another entry holds answers 409 `DuplicateNameError` and nothing is written. `POST /admin/peer-suggestions/dismiss` with the same keys hides the suggestion. Both need `read:settings` and the rights of the catalogue file for the kind: ingredients `manage:ingredients` and `write:ingredients`, units, tags and equipment `write:resources` (403 otherwise). A field that is no suggestion (any more) answers 404 `PeerSuggestionNotFoundError`; an entry the peer never sent or that maps to no local entry answers 404 `PeerMappingNotFoundError`.
5. Suggestions are computed from the mappings on every read, so a newer catalogue entry from the peer updates them at once, also for local entries created from the peer's data (`created_here`). Only dismissals are stored (`peer_suggestion_dismissals`), keyed by peer, kind, the entry's public id at the peer and the field, with a SHA-256 digest of the value the peer offered (references by identity); the suggestion shows again once the peer offers another value. Dismissals go with the pairing.

## 4f food facts

1. Every base ingredient has one to three food facts: short, checkable pieces of trivia (origin, history, botany, production, etymology, the chemistry behind a kitchen effect), never nutrition advice or health claims. They live in `backend/src/bootstrap/data/ingredient_facts.yaml`, one group per ingredient in the order of `ingredients.yaml`, with the English and German Wikipedia article the group draws on (title and the revision the facts were checked against) and per fact a slug `key`, the `source` locale of the article that backs it and the text in en-GB and de-AT, at most 220 characters each. A fact's stable key is `<ingredient key>.<slug>`.
2. Facts are written in our own words and never copied from Wikipedia, whose text is CC BY-SA; the card links the backing article. The en-GB text is British English, the de-AT text uses the Austrian food terms of the catalogue (Erdäpfel, Paradeiser, Topfen) and addresses the reader, if at all, with "du". New or changed facts are reviewed against the cited revision like any catalogue entry ([section 10](#10-base-ingredient-catalogue) item 7.4).
3. Facts are read-only content of the release: they have no table and are not edited, seeded or merged on an instance. The composition root hands them to the cookbook through `IFoodFactSource` (`bootstrap/cookbook_hooks.py`). A fact is shown only while its ingredient, found by seed key, is active; base ingredients cannot be deleted through the API, so in practice an archived ingredient, or one an instance never seeded, retires its facts.
4. `GET /home` carries `food_fact`, the fact of the day, or null when no fact's ingredient is left: the text in the viewer's language (`locale` says which), the ingredient's id and name, and the source article's title and address. Everyone on the instance sees the same fact on the same (UTC) day. The facts are ordered by key and day `d` shows fact `d * stride mod n`, with a stride near `n / 1.618` that shares no factor with `n`, so every fact comes once before any repeats and consecutive days are far apart in the order; when the day's ingredient is gone, the next fact in the order stands in (`domain/services/food_facts.py`). Adding facts reshuffles the rotation once.
5. `tests/unit/test_reference_data.py` checks that every base ingredient has facts in catalogue order, the fields, the lengths, both languages and that every fact's source is one of its group's articles.

## 5 the personal layer

1. Favourites, ratings (one to five stars per user and recipe, and per variation, only on published recipes) and private cook notes belong to their user. The average and count of ratings are public; the own rating and note go to their user only.
2. A food restriction is an allergy or intolerance to one allergen or one ingredient, or a diet (`Diet`: vegetarian, vegan, pescetarian, halal, no pork, no beef, no alcohol) with kind `diet`; exactly one of these is named. Instead of an ingredient id, a restriction may name an ingredient: the catalogue ingredient of that name in any language (ignoring case) is used, or a draft ingredient of that name is created in the user's UI language. This is the only path that creates an ingredient without `write:ingredients`.
3. Food restrictions are health data. They are read and written only through `/users/me/food-restrictions`, for the signed-in user; there is no admin route, no list across users, and they never enter a log line, the security log or an MCP result.
3a. Entering a restriction needs the health data consent (`PUT /users/me/consents/health_data`); without it `POST /users/me/food-restrictions` answers 403 `ConsentRequiredError`. Withdrawing the consent deletes every restriction of the user in the same transaction through the cookbook's `HealthDataEraser` (`application/services/platform_hooks.py`), which the composition root registers with Platform (`bootstrap/cookbook_hooks.py`).
4. Recipe and shopping responses carry warnings computed for the requesting user only (`restriction_rules.warnings_for`); another user's response never reveals them. A diet restriction warns on every ingredient whose markers break the diet, and on every draft ingredient, whose markers nobody has confirmed yet ([section 6a](#6a-diets-and-dinner-guests)).
5. Below the ingredients and steps the recipe page shows four cards in a fixed order: the tips, the reader's own notes, the cook log ("Cooked") and the rating. Each user decides on the account's profile tab which of them they see, through `recipe_show_tips`, `recipe_show_notes`, `recipe_show_cooked` and `recipe_show_rating` on the user (`GET /users/me`, `PATCH /users/me`). All four are on by default, also for accounts that existed before the setting. A card turned off is left out on screen and on paper; the setting only changes what that reader sees and never reaches another user's response. In the same way `recipe_highlight_mentions`, on by default, decides whether the step texts highlight the ingredients, recipes and equipment they mention; turned off, mentions read as normal text and a sub-recipe link keeps only its underline. `keep_screen_on`, also on by default, decides whether the recipe page and the shopping list ask the browser to keep the screen from turning off while they are open (the Screen Wake Lock API); a browser without it, or a page served without HTTPS, lets the screen turn off as usual.
6. How small metric amounts read is the user's choice too (`amount_style` on the user, `GET /users/me`, `PATCH /users/me`, the account's profile tab): `decimals`, the default, keeps them exact to three decimals below one and two below ten (0,125 l, 1,25 kg), and `fractions` shows a kitchen fraction of a litre or kilogram as its glyph (⅛ l, 1 ½ kg), thirds and millilitres staying decimal. Counts read as fractions either way (½ Zitrone). The frontend formats the amounts (`formatAmount` in `features/recipes/utils/scaling.ts`); visitors who are not signed in get the default.

## 6 cooking history and meal plan

1. A cook event records that a user cooked a published recipe, or one of its variations (`variation_id`), on a day, with servings and an optional note. Everyone sees everyone's cook events; each user changes only their own. A cook event cannot lie more than a day in the future.
2. The calendar asks for at most 400 days at once. "On this day" looks three days to either side of today in each of the last five years. A recipe's own cook log (`GET /recipes/{id}/cook-events`, `mine` for the viewer's own) answers the newest 100, or up to 500 with `limit`.
3. The meal plan holds entries per day and meal slot, each a recipe or a free text, with servings. Every user has a personal plan, and a household member also plans in the household's plan ([section 6c](#6c-households)). A range of at most 62 days of one plan can be put on a shopping list in one go, each recipe scaled to its planned servings.
4. `GET /meal-plan/suggestions` (`date_from`, `date_to`, `scope`, optional `slot`, `limit` up to 24) suggests published recipes the viewer may see for a range of one plan, each as a recipe summary with a `reason` (`SuggestionReason`). Candidates come through the same visibility filter as the recipe list; recipes already planned in the range are left out. The history behind the ranking is the plan's: every household member's cook events for the household's plan, the viewer's own for the personal one; only the recipe and its reason leave the use case. The score (`domain/services/meal_suggestions.py`) adds named, bounded signals: how often the scope cooked the recipe (log scaled), "due again" once the time since it was last cooked exceeds its own usual interval, the viewer's favourite, in season in the range's month, cooked within a week of the range's dates in the last five years, and the fit of its seeded meal tags (by seed key) to `slot`; never cooked and recently published recipes get a small boost, which carries the cold start. A recipe cooked in the last three days sinks below every other one, and one tagged only as a component (sauce, side, drink, preserves) or conflicting with the viewer's own food restrictions sinks by a fixed amount. The list is then re-ranked by maximal marginal relevance over tags and ingredients weighted by their rarity, so similar recipes spread out. The reason is the strongest signal relative to its weight. The ranking is deterministic, with ties to the newest recipe.

## 6a diets and dinner guests

1. A recipe fits the diets none of its ingredients' markers breaks (`diet_rules.EXCLUDED`): vegetarian excludes meat, poultry, pork, beef, fish, seafood and gelatin; vegan also dairy, eggs and honey; pescetarian excludes meat, poultry, pork, beef and gelatin; halal excludes pork, gelatin and alcohol; no pork excludes pork and gelatin; no beef excludes beef; no alcohol excludes alcohol. Summaries and the detail carry `diets`; the list filter `diets=` keeps recipes that fit every chosen diet. While a recipe uses a draft ingredient, through its sub-recipes too, it fits no diet (`diet_rules.diets_of`); the detail names those ingredients in `unconfirmed_ingredient_ids`, so the page can say why.
2. A user may opt in to `share_restrictions_with_hosts` on their own profile (`PATCH /users/me`, off by default). Only the user's own `GET /users/me` shows the setting; no admin, directory or recipe response does.
3. The recipe list and detail take `guests`, the ids of active people who are coming (unknown, inactive or service accounts answer 404, the same whether or not they opted in). Each recipe then carries `unsuitable_for_guests`: true when it conflicts with any allergy, intolerance or diet of a guest who opted in. A guest who did not opt in, or who does not hold the health data consent, counts as having no restrictions, so a response never shows whether a guest opted in. `hide_unsuitable_for_guests=true` drops those recipes.
4. Nothing else about the guests leaves `ReadRecipes`: not which guest, not which restriction, no per-guest field, no log line. The host's own `warnings` and `has_warning` stay the host's. With a single guest the flag implies who it concerns, which the UI explains.
5. Filters on derived data (diets, hiding unsuitable recipes) run in memory over at most 5,000 matches. Both also look at the visible variations ([section 3f](#3f-variations-co-editors-and-copies), point 6).
6. The guest check and diets are not offered to agents; `get_recipe` drops `unsuitable_for_guests` with the warnings.

## 6b recipe visibility

1. A published recipe is visible to its owner and, by its `visibility`, narrowest first, to nobody else (`private`), the members of the owner's household (`household`), the chosen users and the members of the chosen groups (`shared`, shares in `recipe_user_shares` and `recipe_group_shares`), every signed-in user of this instance (`instance`), every signed-in user of this instance and every paired instance (`peers`), or anyone on the internet without signing in (`public`). Each level includes the ones before it, so a public recipe goes to every paired instance too. A draft is its owner's alone whatever the visibility (`recipe_rules.can_view_recipe`). A signed-in user reads `instance`, `peers` and `public` recipes alike, in `can_view_recipe` and in the SQL filter; an anonymous visitor reads only published recipes whose effective visibility is `public` ([section 6e](#6e-public-recipes)), so never a `peers` recipe.
2. Private means private for administrators too: the system user and `manage:recipes` only reach recipes they can see. Administrators can still deactivate a user.
3. The privacy settings (`/settings/privacy`, readable by every signed-in user, written with `write:settings`) hold the default visibility of new recipes (`default_recipe_visibility`), whether users may choose (`users_may_change_visibility`) and whether public recipes are allowed (`allow_public_recipes`, off by default). Cuisine is built to share, so a fresh instance runs in peers mode (`peers`, users may not change it, with the `peering` feature on); instance mode, private mode, user choice and public recipes are the administrator's options. These defaults reach only settings that were never saved: an existing instance keeps what it stored, and the migration that introduced them wrote the old defaults (`instance`, peering off) for instances that already had users but had never saved those sections. The dev seed switches to user choice because its demo has private and shared recipes. Locked, every saved recipe gets the default and its shares are cleared; instance mode is `instance` locked, peers mode is `peers` locked (which needs peering to be on), public mode is `public` locked (which needs public recipes to be allowed), private mode `private` locked, and any other locked default is a fixed visibility (`legal_rules.privacy_mode_of`). Unlocked, a new recipe takes the request's visibility, else the owner's own default (`default_recipe_visibility` on `PATCH /users/me`), else the instance default. Switching the settings never rewrites existing recipes.
3a. While `allow_public_recipes` is off, `public` is not offered: setting a recipe to `public` that is not public already, a user's own default to `public` that is not public already, or the instance default to `public` answers 422 `PublicRecipesNotAllowedError`. A stored `public` reads as `instance` meanwhile (`recipe_rules.effective_visibility`, `PrivacySettings.effective_visibility`): a new recipe whose default is `public` starts as `instance`, the sub-recipe warning compares effective visibilities, and the `recipe.published` webhook names the effective one. The row keeps `public`, a save that keeps `public` is accepted, and turning the switch on again restores public access as it was. Recipe responses (summary, detail and editor) carry both: `visibility` is the stored value the owner chose and `effective_visibility` the one that applies, so the owner sees a recipe that is set to public but currently limited to the instance.
3b. `peers` follows the `peering` feature switch the same way. While it is off, `peers` is not offered: setting a recipe to `peers` that is not `peers` already, a user's own default to `peers` that is not `peers` already, or the instance default to `peers` answers 422 `PeerRecipesNotAllowedError`. A stored `peers` reads as `instance` meanwhile (`effective_visibility(..., peering=...)`, `PrivacySettings.effective_visibility(..., peering=...)`), in the responses, the sub-recipe warning, the `recipe.published` webhook, the lock mode and what paired instances receive; the row keeps `peers`, and switching peering on again restores it. Mirrors and copies of mirrors (`peer_capped`) can never be `peers` or `public`: setting either answers 422 `PeerShareNotAllowedError`, and a locked default of either gives them `instance`. Switching the `peering` feature on or off publishes a new privacy statement version, like a change of the privacy settings ([admin-area.md section 14](admin-area.md)).
4. Only the owner changes a recipe's visibility and shares (`can_change_visibility`); a manager's save keeps them. The editor reads `can_change_visibility`.
5. Every read applies it (an `instance`, `peers` or `public` recipe opens to every signed-in viewer, and a public one to anonymous visitors as in [section 6e](#6e-public-recipes)): the list, search and filters (in SQL, `recipe_repository._filtered`), the detail and editor, the home page, the cooking calendar and "on this day" (events of hidden recipes are left out, and stay with their cook), favourites, ratings and notes (only on visible recipes), adding to the meal plan or a shopping list, the dinner guest check and every MCP tool. A meal plan entry or shopping item of a recipe the viewer cannot see keeps its text and loses the link (`recipe: null`).
6. Narrowing a recipe takes away others' access but deletes none of their data; widening it again brings their favourites, notes and cook events back.
7. Groups double as sharing circles: a group without permissions, such as "Family", only decides who sees shared recipes.
8. Whether the author's name appears on a public recipe or on a recipe shared with a paired instance is the author's choice (`show_name_on_public_recipes`, `show_name_on_paired_instances`, [auth.md](auth.md) section 1).
9. Paired instances are share targets inside `shared`, and mirrors of their recipes follow the incoming switch of their peer; both are in [section 6f](#6f-sharing-with-paired-instances-and-mirrors).
10. Usage counts follow the same rule (`ReadResourceUsage`, `usage_repository.py`). `GET /resources/usage` answers, for the resources page alone so that the lists the pickers load stay cheap, every tag, unit, category, family and equipment item with its `usage_count`, zero included; the ingredient list carries `usage_count` on each row and takes `min_usage`, `max_usage` and `sort=usage` (with `order`). Tags, units, equipment and ingredients count the recipes the viewer may see, through the same SQL filter as the recipe list (`recipe_repository.visible_ids`), so the viewer's own drafts count and someone else's private recipe does not. A recipe counts once however many lines use the item: a unit counts the recipes with a step or declared line in that unit or whose yield is given in it, an ingredient those with a step or declared line of it, equipment those whose steps name it. Categories and families count the ingredients in them that the catalogue lists, every one but the archived, since every signed-in user sees the whole catalogue drafts included. Each count is one grouped query per list. The deletion impacts keep their own counts over all recipes, because a hidden recipe still blocks a deletion.

## 6c households

1. A user belongs to at most one household (the primary key of `household_members` is the user). Anyone without one founds one (`POST /households`); a member invites an active person (`POST /households/mine/invitations`), who accepts or declines (`/households/invitations/{id}/accept|decline`); a member may revoke an invitation. Accepting needs the invitee to have left any other household first. There are no roles inside a household.
2. Anyone leaves (`POST /households/mine/leave`); the last member leaving dissolves the household with its meal plan and default list. A leaving member keeps their recipes and cook events; the plan and the list stay with the household.
3. The household owns a shared meal plan: its entries carry its id, and every member reads and changes them. Every user also keeps a personal plan that only they see, including the entries made before joining. `GET /meal-plan`, `POST /meal-plan` and `POST /meal-plan/shopping-list` take `scope` (`household` or `personal`); without it a member means the household's plan and everyone else their own, and `household` without a household answers 422 `NotInHouseholdError`. Changing or deleting an entry needs no scope, since the entry is found in either plan the user reaches, and an update keeps it in its plan. Entry responses carry `scope`.
4. Founding a household creates its default shopping list (`shopping_lists.household_id`), which starts continuous, never deletes itself, every member is on automatically, nobody shares further or deletes, and which goes with the household. Separately shared lists keep working.
5. `GET /households/mine` lists the members, which the recipe browser preselects as dinner guests; the guest check stays as in [section 6a](#6a-diets-and-dinner-guests). Food restrictions stay personal and never become visible to the household.
6. Administrators with `write:users` see who belongs to which household (`GET /admin/households`), never a household's plan or lists.

## 6d deletion

1. Accounts (`DELETE /users/{user_id}`, write:users; `GET /users/{user_id}/deletion-impact` for the dialog) are deleted terminally and without a trace, in one transaction (`DeleteAccountData` inside `ManageUsers.delete`). Credentials, sessions, devices, tokens, the API keys the user created with their service accounts, group memberships, the agent call log, food restrictions, favourites, ratings (averages follow), notes, cook events and the personal plan go; every security event involving the user is removed and one remains, `user_deleted` with the acting administrator and no name, id or detail of the deleted user. Pointers in data that stays become empty. Backups keep the data until they rotate out.
2. The administrator chooses a new owner for the user's recipes and ingredients (`new_owner_id`, any active person) or nobody. Without one, `ownerless_visibility` says what the published recipes do: `keep`, `instance` (`peers` and `public` recipes keep theirs, every other one becomes `instance`) or `delete`; private and household recipes cannot keep theirs (422 `AccountDeletionChoiceError`), since nobody could read them. Deleting is refused (409) when another person's recipe uses one of them as a sub-recipe. Ownerless recipes are editable with manage:recipes only. Drafts are always deleted.
2a. Variations of a deleted account follow the self-deletion rule for published content (`DeleteAccountData.settle_variations`, for both deletions, after the recipes were handed over, kept or deleted): a shared variation of a published recipe visible to every signed-in user (`instance`, `peers` or `public`) stays online without an author ("former member"), unless a self-deleting user chose to delete their public recipes too; private variations and variations of any other recipe are deleted. Ratings of the account's variations by others go with the variation; their cook events keep the recipe.
3. A household loses the member, whose entries of its plan and its default list pass to another member; the last member dissolves it. Lists the user owns pass to the member with the oldest account, or are deleted when nobody else is on them.
4. Recipes (`DELETE /recipes/{id}`, `GET /recipes/{id}/deletion-impact`; the owner or manage:recipes, never a co-editor) are deleted terminally with translations, steps, shares, co-editors, variations, favourites, ratings, everyone's notes, cook events and their images, each stored file only when no other recipe uses it (`RecipeDeletion`). Planned meals become free text with the title; shopping items keep their text without the link; copies lose their `based_on` link. A recipe another recipe uses as a sub-recipe is blocked (409); the impact lists the parents the viewer can see and counts the others.
5. Ingredients (`GET /ingredients/{id}/deletion-impact`): seeded ones are never deleted or merged away (409 `SeededResourceError`). One a recipe uses is blocked (409); the impact gives the count and the recipes the viewer can see, and `POST /ingredients/{id}/merge {into_id}` (manage:ingredients) repoints recipe lines, shopping items and food restrictions before deleting it. An unused ingredient is deleted and its shopping items become free text. When food restrictions name it, it is archived instead (`IngredientStatus.ARCHIVED`): gone from the catalogue, pickers, search and every response, still matched for those users' warnings and named in their own restriction list. Impact, status code and body are the same either way, so deleting reveals nothing about anyone's restrictions.
6. Tags are deleted everywhere without a block. Categories (`DELETE /ingredient-categories/{id}?replacement_id=`) move their ingredients to the replacement or to none, and their shopping items to the seeded "Other". Seeded units are never deleted; a custom unit in use is blocked (`GET /units/{id}/deletion-impact`) and `POST /units/{id}/merge {into_id}` moves recipe lines, yields, shopping items and conversions to another unit. Seeded rows of every kind never come back ([section 9](#9-seeds)).
7. A user deletes their own account with `DELETE /users/me {email, password | passkey{challenge_token, credential}, delete_public_recipes}` (`DeleteOwnAccount`, since Platform's deletion refuses self-deletion); `GET /users/me/deletion-impact` gives the dialog its numbers. The own e-mail address must be typed, in any case (422 `SelfDeletionConfirmationError`), and the password or a passkey assertion over a challenge from `POST /api/v1/auth/login/passkey/options` must verify (403 `PasswordConfirmationError`). Published recipes visible to every signed-in user (`instance`, `peers` or `public`) stay online without an owner, keeping their visibility, or are deleted with `delete_public_recipes` when users may choose visibility; household recipes pass to the remaining member with the oldest account, else they are deleted; private and shared recipes and all drafts are deleted. A recipe or draft that would be deleted but that another user's recipe uses as a sub-recipe is handed over instead (`recipe_handover.handed_over_visibility`): its new owner is the owner of the using recipe, the one with the oldest account when several use it, and nothing in the using recipe changes. Its visibility widens just enough that it keeps every reader it still has and gains the readers of every using recipe, judged by their visibility whether or not they are published: a using recipe visible to every signed-in user (`instance`, `peers` or `public`) makes it an `instance` recipe, never a `peers` or public one, since only an owner sends a recipe to paired instances or publishes it to the internet (a `peers` or public recipe itself keeps its visibility); a household recipe of the new owner's own household makes it a household recipe when nobody else needs adding; otherwise the readers become shares (another using recipe's owner, that recipe's user and group shares, the members of its owner's household), and without any it stays private. Its status stays, so a handed-over draft stays a draft. A recipe that would be deleted but that only recipes staying online without an owner use stays too, without an owner and with its visibility widened by the same rule (those recipes are visible to every signed-in user, so it becomes an `instance` recipe; a draft stays a draft, unread but still counted in the tree's nutrition and allergens). A recipe passing to the household, a recipe handed over and a recipe kept without an owner count as using recipes with their new owner, so the leaving user's sub-recipes below them follow down the tree; only a recipe that no other remaining recipe uses is deleted. The impact lists the hand-overs as `recipes_handed_over` with id, title and the new owner's name and counts the ownerless keeps as `sub_recipes_kept_online`; `recipes_deleted` and `drafts_deleted` leave both out. `if_public_deleted` gives the same four fields for the choice of deleting the public recipes too (null when that choice is not offered), since deleted public recipes may be handed over themselves and change the fates of the recipes below them. Ingredients stay without an owner; the household and the lists follow point 3. Everything personal goes as in point 1, and one `user_deleted` event remains without actor, address or target, its detail `{"self_deletion": true}`. The system user (403 `SystemUserProtectedError`) and the last active holder of `write:users` (409 `LastAdministratorError`) cannot delete themselves; the impact names that as `refused_reason`. Both impacts carry `backup_keep_days` and `backups_clear_by` from `IInstanceFacts.backup_retention()`.
8. `GET /users/me/export` ("download my data") answers one JSON file (`ExportOwnData`, format `cuisine-personal-data` version 1): the profile and consents from Platform's user, the own recipes with status, visibility and the content of a revision snapshot, the own variations with their stored changes (`variations`), owned ingredients, favourites, ratings and cook events (each naming its variation, if any), notes, the plan entries the user made, their shopping lists with items, food restrictions, household (name and member names) and kitchen. A record of a recipe the user can no longer see names it as `{"id": null, "title": null, "accessible": false}`.

## 6e public recipes

1. While `allow_public_recipes` is on, visitors who are not signed in read the published recipes whose effective visibility is `public`, view only, at `/api/v1/public/recipes` (`public_recipes_router.py`, `ReadPublicRecipes`). It is a router set of its own, never optional authentication on the web routes: nothing there reads a session, sets a cookie or touches the personal layer. While the switch is off the list is empty and every recipe, tag list and image answers as unknown.
2. The visitor is a real case of the viewer: `Viewer.visitor(public_allowed=...)` holds a stand-in account with id 0, no permissions and `anonymous` set, `can_view_recipe` answers for it first, and `recipe_repository._filtered` repeats the same rule in SQL through `RecipeQuery.anonymous`. Anything that is not a public recipe answers 404 like an unknown id.
3. `GET /public/recipes` lists and searches them (text search, tags, diets, maximum time, difficulty, newest or by title, at most 48 per page); `GET /public/recipes/tags` names the tags the public recipes carry; `GET /public/recipes/{id}?servings=&locale=` answers the page: title, description, tips, times, difficulty, tags, the derived ingredient list scaled to the servings, the steps with their lines and equipment, the sub-recipes the recipe needs, the equipment, nutrition with the extended nutrients, allergens, diets, seasons and the source. Without `locale` the instance's language applies.
4. The JSON holds exactly what the page shows (`public_recipe_schema.py`): no internal ids, no ids of steps, lines, ingredients, units or equipment, no owner id or e-mail, no visibility or status. The only ids are the recipe's public id, the public ids of its images and of its tags (for the tag filter). Nothing of the personal layer appears: no favourites, ratings or averages, notes, cook events, warnings, guests or kitchen. Variations are not shown on the public page.
5. The author appears only by display name and only when the owner switched `show_name_on_public_recipes` on; otherwise `author` is null. Their avatar follows the name: `author_avatar` is true when the author shows their name and has an avatar, which `GET /public/recipes/{id}/author-avatar` then serves ([file-storage.md](file-storage.md) section 3).
6. A sub-recipe the visitor may not see keeps no title, id or link ([section 3a](#3a-sub-recipes)), and step text mentions are rewritten for the visitor (`public_presenter.step_text`): an ingredient or equipment mention carries its current name and the zero id instead of the catalogue id, a public sub-recipe its title and public id, and a hidden sub-recipe the name `?` and the zero id, which the page shows as "a recipe that is not public".
7. Images come only through `GET /public/recipes/{id}/images/{image_id}`, only for the recipe's cover and step images, only while the recipe is public, and only in display size (the thumbnail); the original is never served, and a file without a thumbnail, of another recipe or of no recipe answers 404 ([file-storage.md](file-storage.md) section 3).
8. Visitors download nothing: no `.cui`, no print view, no export. The public page prints a note to sign in instead of the recipe. There is no schema.org JSON-LD.
9. The surface is rate limited per client address: at most `ANONYMOUS_REQUEST_LIMIT` requests (300) per `ANONYMOUS_REQUEST_WINDOW_SECONDS` (60), JSON and images together (`platform/const.py`, `rate_limit.limit_anonymous_requests` on Redis); above it, 429 with `Retry-After`. Every response carries `X-Robots-Tag: noindex`.
10. Search engines stay away unless the administrator switches `index_public_recipes` on ([admin-area.md](admin-area.md) section 14): only then robots.txt opens the public pages and the pages' robots meta tag says `index, follow`.
11. The resolver (`/resolve/{public_id}`) stays signed-in only; a public page links by the recipe's public id directly.
12. A signed-in user who opens a public page in the web app is sent to the full recipe page.

## 6f sharing with paired instances and mirrors

How recipes go to paired instances and how this instance shows the recipes they share with it. Pairing, the switches per peer and the transport are in [peering.md](peering.md); the payload is a `.cui` file ([formats.md](formats.md)).

Sending:

1. A paired instance is a share target inside `shared`, next to users and groups: `shared_peer_ids` on `RecipeRequest`, stored in `recipe_peer_shares`, answered as `shared_peers` in the editor. Only the owner sets it, like the other shares. Adding a peer needs the `peering` feature switch on, an active pairing and the peer's `users_may_share` switch on (422 `PeerShareNotAllowedError` otherwise, 404 `PeerNotFoundError` for an unknown peer); keeping or removing one always works. `GET /peers/share-targets` names the peers a user may pick (empty while peering is off). The editor's `peer_hidden_sub_recipe_ids` names the direct sub-recipes that do not go to every peer the recipe goes to, for the share dialog's warning.
2. A peer whose `users_may_share` switch is on receives a published recipe when the owner shares it with that peer inside the visibility `shared`, and every published recipe whose effective visibility is `peers` or `public` ([section 6b](#6b-recipe-visibility)); a peer with the switch off receives nothing (`PeerRecipeSharing._audience`). Drafts, recipes without an owner, mirrors and copies of mirrors never leave. Switching `allow_public_recipes` off therefore withdraws the public ones, switching the `peering` feature off withdraws the `peers` ones (sent once peering is on again, which publishes them anew), and a handover to a new owner clears the peer shares (`Recipe.pass_to`), since sharing was the previous owner's choice.
3. The payload (`application/services/peer_sharing.py`, `PeerRecipeSharing`) is a `.cui` file holding the one recipe: its content through the format mapper, `author_name` (the owner's display name only while the owner's `show_name_on_paired_instances` is on) and, under the same rule, `author_avatar`, the key in `files` of the owner's avatar, sent as its thumbnail, its variations that are as visible as the recipe (never a private one, and none for a peer that did not announce the `variations` capability), a catalogue entry for everything it names and its images. A sub-recipe is an item of its own: the payload names it by public id, with its title only when that sub-recipe goes to the same peer. The item's revision, also its etag, is a hash over that payload and the images' content hashes, so it changes with the content, the variations, the images and the sub-recipe titles. The provider checks the audience again when the peer fetches.
4. Every path that changes what a peer receives calls `PeerRecipeSharing.sync` (or `refresh` by public id) inside its own transaction: saving, translating, an image, restoring, publishing and unpublishing, visibility and share changes, deleting (`RecipeDeletion`), deleting an account (handovers, ownerless recipes and deletions), creating, changing, deleting and adopting variations, a peer's `users_may_share` switch, the `allow_public_recipes` setting and the `peering` feature switch (through the peer changed, privacy saved and features saved hooks registered in `src/bootstrap/peering.py`, which call `PeerRecipeSharing.resync`). A new pairing, also one after a revoke, runs the peer paired hook, which calls `PeerRecipeSharing.announce`: the new peer gets `item.shared` for every `peers` and `public` recipe at once, in the pairing transaction on both sides. Since the other instance waits for the handshake's answer meanwhile, these events carry no revision and no payload is built; the peer fetches each recipe anyway, and the next comparison of the recipe records its revision. It compares, per peer, whether the peer should hold the recipe now with the latest event the peer's channel log has for it (`IPeerPublisher.last_published`) and publishes exactly the difference: `item.shared`, `item.updated` with the new revision, or `item.withdrawn`. The recipes using a changed recipe as a sub-recipe are compared too. A change no path reports, such as a renamed catalogue entry, is repaired by the daily comparison.

Receiving:

5. A recipe from a peer first waits for an administrator (`peer_incoming_recipes`, with the received payload and its images), unless the peer's `accept_incoming` switch is off, in which case new recipes are ignored, or its `sync_all` switch is on (item 9a). Nothing of it is visible to users before an administrator accepts it. Updates of a waiting recipe replace the payload kept.
6. Every catalogue entry the recipe and its variations name (units, tags, ingredients, equipment) is matched for that peer: by seed key first, then by the saved mapping of that peer (`peer_catalogue_mappings`), else it needs a decision; a name or alias in either language is offered as a suggestion and never taken silently. The decisions are `map` (to a local entry), `create` (prefilled from the peer's data: ingredients and equipment as drafts for the review queue, ingredients in the chosen category or uncategorised, tags active), `plain` (ingredients and equipment kept as text with the peer's allergens and markers) and `none` (a unit left out, a tag dropped). Units take `map` or `none`, tags `map`, `create` or `none`, ingredients and equipment `map`, `create` or `plain`. A unit that only the conversions of the recipe's ingredients name needs no decision, since no line uses it, but it gets a mapping as well (matched by seed key, otherwise left undecided), so an ingredient created from the peer's data keeps that conversion once the unit is mapped and an administrator can map it by hand in the mappings dialog.
7. `GET /admin/incoming-recipes?status=pending|rejected` (`read:settings`, 404 while peering is off) lists the waiting recipes with peer, title, description, author name, `kind` (`new`, or `update` for a held update of the mirror `mirror_id`), the sub-recipes it uses (title when shared too, whether mirrored or waiting here) and the entries needing a decision with their names, allergens, markers, suggestion and, for ingredients, the suggested category. `POST /admin/incoming-recipes/{id}/accept {decisions: [{kind, source_id, action, target_id, category_id}]}` (`write:settings`) saves the decisions and builds the mirror, or applies the held update, in one transaction; while an entry still lacks a decision it answers 422 `PeerMappingError` with `missing` (`<kind>:<source id>`). `/reject` discards it: it stays as a rejected marker, later updates refresh what is kept of it but never queue it again, and `/reopen` (or accepting it) undoes the rejection. A withdrawn recipe removes the marker too.
8. Every decision is saved as the peer's mapping and reused for its later recipes; the latest catalogue data the peer sent for each entry is kept with the mapping. `GET /admin/peers/{id}/mappings` (`read:settings`) lists them with the action, the local entry, `created_here`, `matched_by` (`seed_key` or `decision`) and how many mirrors use them; `PUT /admin/peers/{id}/mappings/{kind}/{source_id} {action, target_id, category_id}` (`write:settings`, 404 `PeerMappingNotFoundError` for an entry the peer never sent) changes one and rebuilds every mirror of that peer that names the entry from the document it was built from. Merging a local ingredient, unit or equipment item repoints the mappings to the target; a deleted local entry leaves its mapping undecided.
9. An update whose entries all have a mapping applies at once, as a new revision of the mirror. One that brings an entry without a mapping waits as a held update in the same queue, with only the new entries to decide, while the mirror keeps showing its previous revision.
9a. "Sync all from this peer" (`sync_all`, on for a new pairing, kept as stored when an instance pairs again after a revoke, applies while `accept_incoming` is on): a new recipe becomes a mirror at once and an update applies at once, never waiting, because every entry still needing a decision gets the automatic one (`MirrorBuilder.decide_automatically`): ingredients and equipment are mapped to the suggested local entry (seed key, then the saved mapping, then name or alias in either language, taken automatically in this mode), otherwise created from the peer's data as drafts for the review queue, an ingredient in the category the peer names when one matches by seed key or name, else uncategorised (an entry that cannot be created, such as one whose name an archived entry holds, is kept as plain text); units are mapped by seed key or code, otherwise the line keeps no unit; tags are dropped. The decisions are saved as the peer's mappings like an administrator's, so the mappings dialog corrects them. The safety rules stay: the allergen and marker union and unknown markers. A rejected recipe stays rejected and is never accepted this way. Switching `sync_all` on accepts nothing that waits already; `POST /admin/peers/{id}/incoming-recipes/accept` (`write:settings`, 404 while peering is off) accepts every pending recipe of that peer with the automatic decisions in one transaction and answers `{accepted}`, leaving rejected ones alone, and the web app offers it once when the switch goes on.

Mirrors:

10. A mirror is a recipe row without an owner (`mirror_peer_id` set, `recipe_mirrors` with the peer, the source public id, unique together, the etag and source revision, the author name, the document and images it was built from and its plain-text lines), published, created via `peer`, and capped (`peer_capped`). Its visibility is the peer's incoming choice, never wider than `instance`: every signed-in user (`instance`) or the members of the chosen group (`shared` with that group; nobody while the group is gone). So the visibility rule, the SQL filter and `can_view_recipe` apply to mirrors unchanged; changing the peer's switch updates every mirror of that peer. The origin columns name the source instance and recipe.
11. Its steps name the mapped local entries, with step and line ids derived from the source ids as for imports (`step_ids`), so they stay the same across updates; mentions of anything not placed become text. Plain-text lines and equipment and sub-recipe lines whose sub-recipe is not mirrored here are kept in the mirror's extras. The declared ingredient list keeps the lines naming a mapped ingredient or a mirrored sub-recipe and drops the rest; what the steps use of those is still in the extras. Each build appends a revision, so variations on the mirror notice a source change.
12. The ingredient list, nutrition, grams and seasons come from the local catalogue. Allergens and diet markers are the union of the local catalogue and the source's data (`application/services/mirror_facts.py`): inside a peer's mirrors, each local ingredient carries the allergens and markers the peer sent for the entries mapped to it, and plain-text lines carry their own. This holds for the detail, the summaries, the diets filter, the viewer's warnings and the dinner guest check, also for variations and for recipes whose tree holds a mirror. The detail's `source_allergens_added` names the allergens only the source contributed, for the page's notice. An allergen or marker this release does not know is kept and listed in `unknown_allergens` and `unknown_diet_markers`; the recipe then fits no diet, and every allergen (for an unknown allergen) or diet (for an unknown marker) restriction of the viewer or a guest counts as possibly not fitting. Over-warning is the intended failure direction. Adding a mirror to a shopping list adds its catalogue ingredients as for any recipe and each plain-text line as a free-text item named in the reader's language, scaled to the wanted servings like the other lines and joined with an open free-text item of the same text, unit and sub-recipe path; the same holds for mirrors further down a recipe's tree (`ManageShoppingLists._plain_lines`).
13. Responses: summaries carry `mirror {instance_name, author_name, author_avatar_id}` and `peer_capped`; the detail adds `mirror_details` with `peer {id, name}`, `instance_name`, `author_name`, `author_avatar_id`, `source_url` (`<peer base url>/r/<source public id>`, "open at the source"), `source_revision`, `accepted_at`, `updated_at`, `plain_lines` (`step` from 0, `position`, `name`, scaled `amount`, `unit`, `note`, `allergens`, `diet_markers`), `plain_equipment` and `unlinked_sub_recipes` (`title` only when the source shares that sub-recipe with this instance). Variations carry `mirrored`, `source_author_name` and `source_author_avatar_id`. The avatars a source sends are stored as files of the mirror (their local ids in the mirror's extras), open for whoever may see the mirror ([file-storage.md](file-storage.md) section 1), and are deleted with the mirror or when an update no longer sends them. MCP's tools show the same ([mcp-tools.md](mcp-tools.md)).
14. A mirror is read only. Saving, translating, images, restoring and reading the history, publishing, unpublishing, deleting, visibility, shares and co-editors answer 409 `MirrorReadOnlyError` for anyone who can see it (`recipe_rules.assert_not_mirror`); `can_edit_recipe` and `can_delete_recipe` are false. A mirror is never linked as a sub-recipe of a local recipe (422), since it may vanish. Its variations that came from the source are read only too (409 `MirrorReadOnlyError` for changing or deleting them) and are never adopted. The personal layer works as on any recipe and stays here: favourites, notes, ratings, cook events, the meal plan and shopping.
15. Variations: the source's variations come with the mirror under ids derived from theirs, mapped through the same catalogue mapping (an unmapped swap ingredient holds the update like any new entry), keeping only the changes that fit, with `source_author_name` (the author's display name at the source, or none). Local users with `write:recipes` who can see the mirror add their own variations as on any recipe; they stay here, never go to the source and nobody adopts them.
16. "Copy as my recipe" on a mirror makes a fork: an independent local recipe linked by `based_on`, with `peer_capped` set for good (copies of the copy inherit it). A capped recipe is never `peers` or `public` (422 `PeerShareNotAllowedError`, and a `peers` or `public` default reads as `instance` for it) and never gets peer targets. The fork turns plain-text lines and sub-recipe lines naming a mirror into text at the end of their step, since a local recipe names only catalogue ingredients and local recipes.
17. Sub-recipes: each is accepted on its own. A mirror whose sub-recipe is not mirrored yet lists that line under `unlinked_sub_recipes`; accepting the sub-recipe later links it into every mirror of that peer using it, and withdrawing it unlinks it again.
18. Withdrawn (unshared, narrowed, unpublished, deleted, its owner deleted, the peer's `users_may_share` or the source's `peering` or `allow_public_recipes` switch turned off) or reconciled away, a recipe removes its mirror and anything waiting. The mirror is deleted like any recipe ([section 6d](#6d-deletion)): its local variations, favourites, ratings, notes and cook events go with it, planned meals become free text and shopping items keep their text; forks stay, losing their `based_on` link. Revoking the pairing, from either side, removes every mirror, waiting recipe and catalogue mapping of that peer (the cookbook's revoke hook); forks stay.
19. A mirror counts as in use for the deletion rules of ingredients, units and equipment ([section 6d](#6d-deletion)), since its steps name them.
20. A peer that gains a capability on renegotiation: every mirror and waiting recipe of that peer forgets its etag, and the next full comparison (made due at once) fetches them again.
21. A mirror is never passed on: exporting it as a `.cui` file answers 409 `MirrorReadOnlyError`, since a `.cui` file would forward a recipe its home instance shared only with this one, and the administrator's bulk export leaves mirrors out, also when one is chosen by id or used as a sub-recipe. "Copy as my recipe" keeps a recipe within this instance; whether a fork may be exported is not restricted yet.
22. In the web app, the share dialog (`VisibilityPicker.vue`) offers the paired instances from `GET /peers/share-targets` inside `shared`, next to people and groups, and hides them while there are none; it warns with the names from `peer_hidden_sub_recipe_ids` while instances are chosen, and for a capped recipe it offers neither `peers`, `public` nor instances and says why. The `peers` level ("Paired instances") sits between `instance` and `public`, is offered while peering is on (from `GET /api/v1/public/info` features), and a stored `peers` keeps the option with a note that it reads as everyone here meanwhile. The recipe page shows no visibility badge; the owner sees and changes the visibility in the share dialog. A refused save (`PeerShareNotAllowedError`, `PeerNotFoundError`) reloads the targets. Mirrors carry a badge and "by <author> · <instance>" (only the instance without an author) on cards and on the page; on the page the badge opens a dialog that explains the mirror and links "Open at <instance>" to `source_url` in a new tab. The page offers no editing, publishing, deleting, co-editors or export, and keeps "Copy as my recipe" with a note that the copy stays here. The page and cook mode put plain lines and unlinked sub-recipes back into their steps by `step` and `position` (`features/recipes/utils/mirrors.ts`) and into the ingredient list as text, add plain equipment, name the allergens the source added, list unknown allergens and markers as "contains: X (unknown marker)" and tell dinner guests that the recipe might not fit. The administrator's side is in the Peers tab: "Incoming recipes" (`AdminIncomingRecipesSection.vue`, with the decision dialog `IncomingRecipeDialog.vue`) and each peer's catalogue mappings (`PeerMappingsDialog.vue`).

## 7 shopping lists and live updates

1. A list has an owner and members; all of them read and change its items. Only the owner renames members, shares or deletes the list; a member may leave it.
2. An item is a catalogue ingredient (grouped under its category, in the category's aisle order) or a free text. A new amount of an ingredient joins an open item of the same ingredient and unit (`shopping_rules.find_mergeable`); adding a recipe adds its derived list that way, and the recipe joins the item's recipes. Item responses list them in `recipes`, first one first: a recipe the viewer cannot see is a placeholder without id and title (`accessible: false`), a deleted one is left out, and `recipe` stays the first one or null.
3. Every change publishes `{type, list_id, by}` on the Redis channel `cuisine:shopping-list:<list id>` after the commit. `GET /shopping-lists/{id}/events` streams it as server-sent events: `ready` on connect, then `change`. A client refetches the list on every change and after every reconnect; the payload carries no item data.
4. A stream releases its database connection before it starts and ends after 30 minutes, so the reconnect checks the session and the membership again.
5. Every list has a `mode`, `continuous` or `normal`, which anyone on the list switches with `PUT /shopping-lists/{id} {mode}`. A household's default list is created continuous; every other new list starts normal. Every add, tick, edit, item removal, rename, sharing change, mode change and keep change sets `last_activity_at` (`ManageShoppingLists._touch` and `update`).
6. Continuous: a ticked item stays on the list, and its `checked_at` says when it was ticked; the UI shows ticked items as "Recently bought" with undo (unticking). `bought_window_hours` hours after the tick the purge deletes it; a ticked item without `checked_at` counts as older. Switching a list to continuous at once deletes the items ticked before the window and publishes `items_changed`.
7. Normal: the list keeps every item. When nobody has touched it for `idle_list_days` days the purge deletes it, unless a member set `keep` (`PUT /shopping-lists/{id} {keep}`) or it is a household's default list (whatever its mode); 0 days turns this off. List responses carry `mode`, `keep`, `last_activity_at` and `deletes_on`, the UTC day of the deletion or null when the list never deletes itself (`shopping_modes.deletes_on`); the full list also carries `bought_window_hours`. Switching keep off counts as activity, so a long untouched list never vanishes the moment keep goes off.
8. The purge (`PurgeShoppingLists`) runs every 15 minutes from `src/bootstrap/shopping_jobs.py`, like the nightly jobs: each worker checks, and the first to find it due claims the run in the `job_state` settings row. It commits, then publishes `items_changed` for each continuous list it trimmed and `list_deleted` for each list it deleted, with `by: null`. Running it twice changes nothing the second time.
9. When an idle list deletes itself, the owner and every member get a row in `shopping_list_notes` (list name, idle days, time). These are per-user notes rather than admin notices, because notices are broadcast to everyone, managed by an administrator and acknowledged once: a note concerns one person and one list. `GET /shopping-lists/notes` lists the viewer's own, newest first, and `DELETE /shopping-lists/notes/{id}` dismisses one (404 for anyone else's). The notes go with the user's account.
10. The window and the idle days are the admin settings section `shopping` (`GET/PUT /settings/shopping`, read and write settings), shown in the General tab.

## 8 nutrition lookup

1. Nutrition is entered by hand or prefilled from USDA FoodData Central (public domain, CC0) through `GET /nutrition/usda/search` and `GET /nutrition/usda/{fdc_id}`, which only holders of a write or manage ingredient permission may call.
2. Only the generic foods (Foundation, SR Legacy) are searched. Values are per 100 g; sodium becomes salt as sodium × 2.5. The UI names the source as "USDA FoodData Central".
3. The lookup is switched on and keyed in the integration settings (`/settings/integrations`); the key is encrypted at rest and defaults to `DEMO_KEY`, which allows only a few requests per hour.
4. The German BLS database is not integrated, because it has no licence text that covers redistribution.

## 9 seeds

1. All production reference data lives in `backend/src/bootstrap/data/`: the hand-edited `units.yaml`, `categories.yaml`, `tags.yaml`, `families.yaml`, `ingredients.yaml`, `ingredient_facts.yaml` ([section 4f](#4f-food-facts), read at runtime and never seeded into tables) and `equipment.yaml`, and the machine-written `ingredients.nutrition.json`. `bootstrap/reference_data.py` loads and merges them; the startup seed, the dev seed and the validation test all read them through it.
2. Every row of every kind has a stable `key`, stored as the row's `seed_key`. A row the seed inserts gets the deterministic public id of its kind and key; an adopted row keeps its own ([persistence.md section 5](persistence.md#5-public-ids-the-resolver-and-the-instance-id)). On every start (`bootstrap/cookbook_seed.py`), in one transaction: a key that was never seeded is inserted and remembered in `seeded_keys` together with the values the seed wrote, its merge base; a remembered key is never inserted again, so a row an administrator deleted stays deleted. A row that predates the keys and matches one (a unit with the key as its code, a category, tag, family, ingredient or equipment item with one of the key's names) is adopted: its key is remembered with the seed's values as base and nothing is inserted.
2a. Seed updates are merged per field (`bootstrap/seed_merge.py`): each translation and alias list per locale, category, family, every nutrition value and extended nutrient, density, conversions, allergens, diet markers, season months, unit factors, tag kind and colour, equipment kind, icon and basic flag. When the seed's value differs from the base and the row still holds the base, the row takes the seed's value; when the row already holds it, only the base follows; when the row differs from both, the edit stays and the field is an overridden update. A seed name or alias that another catalogue entry holds is not written either. Rows that left the seed are no longer updated. A key remembered before bases existed gets the seed's values as base, so nothing changes silently.
2b. `GET /admin/reference-updates` (read:settings) lists the overridden updates with the current, seed and base values, allergens and diet markers first; `POST /admin/reference-updates/accept {kind, key, field}` (write:settings) writes the seed's value (404 when the field holds no overridden update).
3. Ingredients name their category and units by key. A category or unit that is gone (deleted, or renamed beyond recognition before it had a key) leaves the ingredient uncategorised or without that conversion.
4. Seeded ingredients have no owner, are active, are written in en-GB first and carry `nutrition_source` usda with their FDC id where FDC supplies the values.
5. `make seed-dev` runs the same reference seed, then adds demo data only: the accounts anna (in the default groups Cooks and Kitchen helpers, recreated if they were deleted) and ben, the household of admin and anna with its plan and list, five recipes (one private, one shared with Cooks, homemade lasagna sheets with a 500 g yield used by a lasagna), a year of cook events, a meal plan week, a list shared with ben, and the opt-ins and restrictions for the dinner guest check. It creates no ingredients, units, tags or categories; its recipes use base ingredients by key, and an everyday ingredient it lacks is added to `ingredients.yaml`. It runs in debug mode only and does nothing when recipes exist (`backend/scripts/seed_dev.py`).
6. `GET /nutrition/sources` returns the credit line per locale, the citation and the licence of the seeded nutrition, for the UI to show.

## 10 base ingredient catalogue

1. Every instance starts with about 620 everyday ingredients, the entries of `ingredients.yaml`. Each has a stable `key`, names in both content languages (Austrian terms in de-AT), a category key, allergens, diet markers, and optionally the season months (`season`), a density and grams per count unit (by unit key). The extended nutrients come from FDC through `ingredients.nutrition.json` (`extended`).
2. Nutrition comes from USDA FoodData Central, Foundation Foods where that profile is complete and SR Legacy otherwise. The data is in the public domain (CC0); no attribution is required, but the UI credits it with the credit line of `GET /nutrition/sources`: "Nutrition data: USDA FoodData Central (public domain)." / "Nährwerte: USDA FoodData Central (gemeinfrei)."
3. `scripts/build_base_ingredients.py` reads `ingredients.yaml` and never writes it. It downloads the FDC datasets into a cache outside the repository (`~/.cache/cuisine/fdc`, or `--cache-dir`), looks up every entry's food and writes `ingredients.nutrition.json`, keyed by ingredient key: the nutrition per 100 g (FDC's values, gaps filled from the entry's own `nutrition`), the FDC id and dataset, and the density and conversions FDC portions yield where the YAML sets none. It reports foods that changed their description and values FDC lacks; `--check` exits 1 when the JSON is out of date.
4. To add an ingredient, append an entry to `ingredients.yaml` with a new key, both names, the category key, allergens, diet markers and the `fdc_id` of the matching generic food (raw or unprepared where the food has such a form), then rebuild, review the JSON diff and commit both files. What the YAML sets wins over the JSON when the seed merges them. An entry marked `proxy` borrows a similar food's nutrition because FDC has no match (porcini from white mushrooms); an entry with `nutrition` and no `fdc_id` carries its own values.
5. Values FDC leaves out stay unknown, except those that follow from the others: no sugar or fibre without carbohydrates, no saturated fat without fat. About 70 entries miss single values, mostly the sugar of grains, herbs and spices; recipes using them show their nutrition as incomplete.
6. Diet markers and allergens are curated by hand and are what the diet filters and allergy warnings rely on. `tests/unit/test_reference_data.py` checks the merged data: unique keys, both languages for every unit, category, tag and ingredient, known categories, units, allergens and markers, only known fields in `ingredients.yaml`, plausible densities, and nutrition that agrees with its energy. It also checks the rules that follow from what a food is: every meat is marked meat or poultry, every fish fish or seafood, every dairy product dairy or eggs (plant drinks aside), pork and beef are also meat, and each member of a family declares what the family implies (cheese milk and dairy, pork pork, wine and spirits alcohol, flour, pasta, bread and pastry gluten, with the gluten free members listed by name).
7. Curation rules, for people and agents alike:
   1. Allergens and markers describe what products in an Austrian supermarket contain. Where common brands differ, the entry declares what any common brand contains (alcohol in teriyaki because the leading brands use wine, beef in kabanos because Cabanossi often has it), since a needless warning costs less than a missed one. Traces ("may contain") are not declared. A composite product (pastes, sauces, sausages, sweets, ready pastry) is checked against several current labels before its allergens are set, and soy lecithin, barley malt, mustard seed and sulphite preservatives count.
   2. The main de-AT name is the Austrian term (Paradeiser, Topfen, Obers); German German and regional names are aliases. The main en-GB name is British English, US names are aliases. An alias names the same food, never a related one.
   3. The FDC match is the generic raw or unprepared form of the same food. A proxy is acceptable when the stand-in is the same kind of food in the same state (fresh for fresh, dried for dried) and close in energy and macronutrients; otherwise the entry carries its own `nutrition` from typical labels. Either way `source_note` says which food stands in or where the values come from, and why that is close enough.
   4. A batch of new or changed entries is reviewed by someone who did not write it before it is merged, allergens and markers first, and the calls that depend on the product (such as shrimp paste in curry paste) are listed in the pull request.
   5. An administrator's edit to a seeded field shows up in `GET /admin/reference-updates` once the seed changes that field; edits that keep recurring there are a sign the seed is wrong and are worth taking back into `ingredients.yaml`.
